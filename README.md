# Emberfront

A fast, arcade-paced browser territory war: settle open land with gold, then make alliances or send your soldiers by land or sea to conquer your neighbours, while every kingdom grows its own villages, farms, mines, towers and roads.

## Running it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # play at http://localhost:5173 with live reload
npm test           # simulation regression test (no browser needed)
npm run build      # type-check and build a static site into dist/
```

`dist/` can be hosted anywhere that serves static files (GitHub Pages, Netlify, itch.io). It uses relative paths, so it works from a subfolder.

## How the code is organised

```
src/
  core/      constants, colour/number helpers, seeded random & noise, countries and their flags
  sim/       the game itself: map generation, territory, buildings, people, research, war & diplomacy, the sea (naval.ts), bots
  render/    camera, terrain & territory pixels, building, ship, nature & people sprites, effects, frame drawing
  ui/        HUD, log & toasts, buildings & research panels, diplomacy & war chips, mouse/touch/keyboard input
  game.ts    connects the simulation to the screen (new game, spawn, player orders)
  main.ts    boot and the main loop
tests/       scripted matches checked against recorded results
tools/       realmaps.ts: draws the real-world maps from Natural Earth; dem.py: Europe's elevation for it; regions.ts: their provinces
```

**The one rule: `src/sim` never touches the page.** No `document`, no canvas, no `Math.random`. The simulation:

- runs in fixed 0.1 s ticks and gets all of its randomness from the map seed, so the same seed plus the same player orders always produce the same match;
- takes player actions only as **commands** (`queueCommand`), which are applied at the start of the next tick and re-checked there;
- reports what happened through **events** (`sim/events.ts`: tile changed, building placed, capital fell, …). Rendering and UI subscribe to these; with no subscribers (a server, a test) the simulation runs the same, just without visuals.

That split is what makes online multiplayer possible later: every browser runs the same simulation and only commands go over the network.

**Nothing scans the whole map once a second any more.** Which kingdoms share a border (and how long it is) and the middle of each kingdom (where its label goes) are counted once at the start of a match (`scanContacts` in `sim/census.ts`) and then kept exact as each tile changes hands (`setOwner` in `sim/territory.ts`); a test checks they always match a fresh count. On Europe's 3.5 million tiles that alone saved a tenth of the simulation's time.

**Keeping the late game fast.** Measured on Europe with 63 rivals (a headless profile of 300 ticks at 2,000 to 40,000 ticks in), a tick costs 2.6 ms early, 24 ms at 17 minutes and 70 ms at 33 minutes; the game needs 50 ms to keep real time (20 ticks a second), and the cost per person triples as territories and wars grow. The drawing costs 3 to 4 ms a frame and doesn't grow. What was done about it, none of it changing how anything plays: **a front's line** (`lineOf` scanned the whole kingdom's box every 5 ticks for every front, the biggest single cost late on) is now kept for up to 20 ticks and worked out again at once whenever land is occupied or freed, orders change, or a strip is about to be taken (`LINE_EVERY`, `occSig`, `redeploy` in `sim/war.ts`); the tower-safe line of defending armies is kept 60 ticks; **the mob check** looks only at the soldiers of kingdoms at war with the one it checks instead of everyone on the map for every kingdom; `updateWar` makes two passes over the people instead of five, and healing is done a tenth of the men at a time (ten times as much); **fighters keeping room** (`spread`) uses a fixed hash grid instead of making a map each tick; **the census** (`censusStep` in `sim/census.ts`) counts a tenth of the plots each tick and swaps the finished count in every tenth, so no tick pays for all of it (the counts are up to 20 ticks old; `census()` still counts at once for a test or a command); and **the game loop** (`STEP_BUDGET` in `main.ts`) runs ticks for at most 10 ms of a frame (always one if due) and lets go of a backlog it can't catch up on, so an overloaded game runs slower rather than freezing the picture. Result: 51 ms down to 34 ms a tick at 30,000 ticks and 69 down to 60 at 40,000, the slowest tick from 500 ms to about 140, and the early spikes (the census) gone.

## Maps and kingdoms

**New map** on the start card goes round two maps (`MAPS` in `game.ts`): **Emberland**, a small generated map of one landmass, always the same, and **Europe**, the real continent (the smaller of the two that were drawn: the others are still in the code for the tests).

The real-world maps (`sim/realmap.ts`) are drawn from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain): its coastlines, islands and lakes, its deserts, and (but for Europe) its named mountain ranges and plateaus. Each is framed on a bigger grid than a generated map's 400×248, so close capitals have room to grow (`sim/mapview.ts`): the Balkans and Anatolia (560×348 tiles) from the Adriatic to the Caucasus, and the world (640×396) cut through the Bering Strait. The map's size is a live setting (`setGrid` in `core/constants.ts`, `resizeGrid` in `sim/state.ts`): everything sized by the map is made anew when it changes. `tools/realmaps.ts` turns the data into a compact grid of ground kinds in `sim/maps.ts` (`node tools/realmaps.ts <folder of Natural Earth GeoJSON> [map ids]`; it lists the files it needs, and redraws only the maps named). On the Balkans and the world, islands too small to matter are dropped and the rest are joined to the mainland by **land bridges** (sandbars) across the narrowest straits: Dover, the Bosporus, Messina, Korea, Torres and so on (a few drawn by hand: Java along the Lesser Sundas to Australia, and the Americas by Baffin Island, Greenland, Iceland and the Faroes to Scotland), so every kingdom can reach every other.

**Europe is the big one**, made for full 64-kingdom games, and drawn with the most care: **1824×1488 tiles**, a tile about 3 km, twice the scale of the other maps and 30 generated maps' worth, from Iceland to Azerbaijan (the map ends on the Caspian shore, a little past Moscow, with no Kazakhstan and only the west of Russia) and from the Arctic coast to the Nile delta, so 62 countries have their capital on it, from Portugal, Morocco and Egypt to Georgia and Azerbaijan (Iran's Tehran lies just past the edge). With a full game of them each has twice the land of a 10-kingdom generated game, so there is plenty of room to play, and seeing it all means panning about. **The sea is as it is**: no land bridges, so Britain, Ireland, Iceland, the Mediterranean islands and the Aegean's stand on their own (a ship will have to cross; the countries founded there can't reach the mainland yet), drawn at 1:10 million with the minor islands too, Natural Earth's finest lakes (Finland's and the Alps' among them), only specks under 12 tiles and ponds under 6 dropped, and every island with a capital on it kept however small (Malta). **Its relief is real**: mountains, hills and uplands come from elevation (the Terrarium tiles of SRTM, GMTED and ETOPO data, resampled tile by tile by `tools/dem.py`), not from named ranges' outlines: ground above a snow line that falls from 3000 m in the south to 900 m in the far north is high (snow, ringed by rock), mountains are above 70% of it or where the land rises steeply, hills where it rises and falls, and upland plateau where it is high but level, such as the Meseta and Anatolia; then each tile takes the kind most of its neighbours have, so ranges are solid. So the Alps, the Pyrenees, the Carpathian arc, the Dinarides, the Scandinavian fjells and their fjords, the Caucasus are where and what they are, and the plains are plains. Deserts still come from Natural Earth, their outlines frayed into dry land at the edges.

On every real map capitals lie in valleys and on plains: around each one the ranges give way, and a capital by the sea with no room for a castle gets a small harbour filled in. Elsewhere the climate goes smoothly by latitude and distance from the sea: rainforest at the equator, dry subtropics, temperate grass and woods, northern forest, then tundra and ice, in patches of gradient noise turned a little each octave so no grid shows through, as big in tiles on Europe as on the smaller maps (and on Europe, desert only south of 36°N).

**Every kingdom is a real country** (`core/countries.ts`), with its name and its flag, drawn as 12×8 pixel art. The flag gives the kingdom its colour, flies on its leaders' standards and its armies' banners (halved to 6×4), and marks it in the panels and on the leaderboard. **Choosing countries.** The start card shows your country and how many rivals you face; clicking it opens the **Countries** window (`ui/nations.ts`). It lists every country that can play on the map (all of them on every map: on a real-world one, a country with no capital on the map is founded at a random place). Click one to play it, and tick the countries you want to face, or use the quick picks: the nearest 5, 10 or 20 capitals (at random on a generated map), the **standard match**, a **full game**, or none. **The standard match is 24 kingdoms on every map** (`STANDARD_KINGDOMS` in `core/constants.ts`, the *Standard · 24* button): you and 23 rivals drawn at random, afresh for each game, and it is what a map starts with. On Europe the rivals with a capital on the map are drawn first, and are founded where *Rivals start* says: **at their capitals** or at **random places** (or random places close together); on the generated Emberland they are founded at random places around the map. **A game holds at most 64 kingdoms** (`MAX_KINGDOMS` in `core/constants.ts`): you and 63 rivals. *Full game · 64* fills it, choosing the 63 at random when more countries could play; on a map with fewer it reads *All*, and ticking a rival past the limit is refused. **Europe holds 24** (`EUROPE_KINGDOMS`, `kingdomLimit` in `core/constants.ts`: a size its late game runs smoothly at, found by profiling): you and **23 rivals drawn at random from the other 61 countries**, afresh for each game (the button reads *Random 23*). All 62 countries stay open to play as, and you can still tick your own rivals (up to 23) or use *Nearest 5/10/20*; a choice of your own is kept for the next game instead of being drawn again. **A small Europe** (*Europe (small, for testing)*, `europe-s`) is the same Europe at two thirds of the size each way (1216×992, 4/9 of the tiles; a tile is about 4.6 km), drawn again from the same ground (`of` in `sim/mapview.ts`), with room for the standard 24 kingdoms too (it was 16; this is the Europe the game plays). **Where the rivals start** (a real-world map, in the country window): *At their capitals* (as ever), *Random places* (each rival keeps its country but is founded anywhere on land with room) or *Random, close together* (random places, but each never more than 7% of the map's width from another kingdom, so everyone has neighbours: `Placement`, `NEAR_FAR` in `sim/sim.ts`). The choice is remembered for each map. On a real-world map, **Start as** founds you at once at your country's real capital, and every rival at its own (close capitals push each other a little apart). On a generated map you still choose where to found your capital, and the rivals are founded around the map. Countries with nearly the same flag colour are told apart by a shade. **The game's speed.** There is one speed (no speed button): the simulation runs two of its seconds in each real second (`GAME_SPEED` in `core/constants.ts`). What the screen shows in time is in real seconds: the clock, *Next human in*, the wage countdown, the income per second and its breakdown, the goods in and out per minute, and the research points a second. The simulation, its tests and the descriptions below count in its own seconds, so a figure like "every 30 s" below is 15 s on the clock.

**Big matches.** A full 64-kingdom game on Europe loads in about a second and runs at 60 frames a second at the game's speed. What makes that possible: each kingdom keeps the box its land lies in, so pushing a border looks only there (`setOwner`, `startAttack` in `sim/territory.ts`); each kingdom knows which goods its land still holds, and a villager with nothing to gather waits a few seconds before looking again (`nearestResources`, `think` in `sim/population.ts`); and the map's pixels go to the screen in 256-pixel squares, only the ones that changed (`flushWorld` in `render/terrain.ts`). The camera never zooms out past the whole map, and keeps it on screen with only a little sea past its edges (`clampCam` in `render/camera.ts`).

**Capitals in their own tradition** (`render/capitals.ts`). Each country builds its capital in its region's style, in all three castle tiers and two states of battle damage. There are 28 of them: the Western keep, Central European baroque, Hanseatic brick, Nordic timber halls, Mediterranean towers, Orthodox domes, Greek whitewash and (at tier 3) a marble temple, the Kremlin, Ottoman domes and minarets, Maghreb kasbahs, Arabian mud forts, a Mesopotamian ziggurat, Egyptian pylons and pyramids, Persian tilework, Mughal marble, Sahel mud mosques, Ethiopian Gondar castles, southern African stone enclosures, Chinese gate towers (in Vietnamese and Korean colours too), Japanese keeps, Siamese and Burmese temples, a Javanese candi, steppe gers, Mesoamerican pyramids, Inca stonework, Spanish colonial cathedrals, the Capitol, and Gothic parliaments. They grow taller and gain towers and domes at tier 2 and gold at tier 3, and every one but the keep flies its country's flag. Damage is worked out from the drawing itself, so it fits any shape: knocked-off tops, cracks, soot, breaches, fires and rubble.

## How it looks

The simulation works in 2 pixels per tile (where people stand). The renderer draws everything at twice that detail (`K`, `RS`, `PS` in `core/constants.ts`): 4×4 pixels per tile, 16×16 per building plot, people 4×8. The camera works in these render pixels. Drawing never changes the simulation, so the look can change freely without touching the determinism test.

- **Tiles of the picture** (`render/tiles.ts`). The map is drawn in square tiles of 256 render pixels (64 map tiles), each with a canvas for the ground, one for nature and one for buildings. A tile is made and painted from the game's state only when it comes on screen (four a frame at most, nearest the middle first, the overview standing in meanwhile), changes are painted into the tiles that exist, and those seen longest ago are let go past a hundred, so the picture costs what the screen shows whatever the map's size: the big Europe holds 60 frames a second in a desktop browser and on a phone, at every zoom. Zoomed far out (a map tile under 2.5 screen pixels), the **overview** is drawn instead: one pixel a map tile, with the ground's colours, the kingdoms and their borders, occupied land, roads, and towns as their roofs.
- **Terrain** (`render/terrain.ts`): a warm, slightly muted palette lit from the top-left; sea with wave dashes, a foam line and wet sand; grass tufts and the odd flower. Kingdoms get a double border: their colour on the edge and a paler line inside. Roads are two-pixel tracks with worn verges.
- **Sprites** (`render/sprites.ts`, `render/nature.ts`, `render/people.ts`) are hand-placed pixel art, many drawn from text grids (`render/pixel.ts`), and outlined automatically where they meet open ground, so towns and crowds stay readable. Buildings and trees are drawn into the tiles of the picture as they are made, and again where they change; people are made once per look and stamped each frame.
- **People** face the way they walk and step as they go. Soldiers wear a tabard and helmet crest in their kingdom's colour and raise a spear, thrust forward on the tick they strike. Each figure is outlined in a dark shade of its kingdom's colour, so mixed crowds stay apart.

## Gold and strength

Each kingdom starts with 250 gold, **10 wood, 10 stone and 10 copper**, and the land within 8 tiles of its castle (`GOODS_START` in `core/constants.ts`, `START_R` in `sim/sim.ts`), and earns from two taxes: **0.1 gold a second from each housed villager** (0.6 and 1.1 with taxation research: +0.5 a tier, `TAX_PER_TIER`; soldiers, the homeless and beggars pay none) and a small **land tax of 0.0004 gold a second for every tile it holds** (`LAND_TAX` in `core/constants.ts`: 2 a second for 5,000 tiles, **doubled during the land rush**, a flat boost for size that makes land worth having beyond the room to build); each coin minter adds 10% to both. **Soldiers are paid wages by what they are doing** (`wageOf` in `sim/war.ts`): **0.22** a second each idle in a barracks (a low-maintenance rate), **0.3** on a border (deployed in peace, massed in a crisis, or given the defend order at war) and **0.4** on the offensive. Militia (below) cost nothing at home, **0.1** called up to a border or defending and **0.2** on the offensive. **Land the enemy occupies pays no land tax.** The treasury can go below zero (the gold turns red); nobody signs up as a soldier while it is, and after 10 seconds unpaid, soldiers start laying down their arms where they stand, one every 3 seconds, the least armoured first (those at home before those away). A deserter's armour is scrapped for 30 gold per tier (60 for reinforced, 90 for heavy). The kingdom card breaks the income down: villager tax, land tax, what the coin minters add, what the soldiers cost and what the militia cost while they are called up (`incomeParts` in `sim/sim.ts`), and counts down when wages go unpaid. Bots keep only as many soldiers as their tax can pay, and at war also what their treasury can pay over a minute and a half (tax alone keeps only about a quarter of a kingdom under arms, since a soldier costs three times what a villager pays). **Settling spreads as a front, never all at once**: the push takes tiles from its edge at random, a little sooner those the border has wound round (up to four tried, the one with most of your land about it taken), so holes fill as it goes, at the usual price and pace. Capturing pockets instantly is for war occupation alone (`encircle` in `sim/war.ts`). Clicking unclaimed land opens a small menu right there: push the border toward it with 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the treasury (keys 1 to 4): 0.36 gold a tile. Rough ground settles a little slower, by `TMULT` in `core/constants.ts`, cut by 80% so settling anywhere pays: mountains ×1.4 (were ×3), snowfields ×1.28 (×2.4), hills ×1.16 (×1.8), forest ×1.08, desert ×1.04, beaches ×1.02. Hovering unclaimed land shows it. Unspent gold comes back when a push ends. Gold never buys another kingdom's land: that takes war (below).

**Strength** is what the labels and leaderboard show, and it is **military might alone**: **100** for each villager and each militiaman, and for a soldier **200, 300 or 500** by his armour tier (I, II, III): land and gold count for nothing, and nor do beggars (`STR_*` in `core/constants.ts`, `might` in `sim/population.ts`).

## People

The capital releases one human every 20 seconds, 3% sooner per farm and **0% to 200% sooner by twice the share of its beds that stand empty, plus 0.5% sooner for every bed it has** (100 beds: +50%, empty or full; carpenters make beds fast): the beds its houses hold (a hovel holds 1, a stone house 2, a manor 3) beyond the people it has, over all of them. With 12 people and 12 beds the bonus is 0; with 6 people it is 100%; with 10 hovels and 1 villager, 180%. So even a small kingdom early in the game that has lost its people recovers fast, and a builders guild that raises houses a tier adds beds (`humanRate`, `houseBonus` in `sim/population.ts`; the top-left panel shows *farms +x% · empty homes +y%*). **Every newcomer is a villager**, coming out of the castle's gate (barracks raise nobody). **Soldiers are trained at a barracks**: the kingdom keeps the share under arms its slider asks for, as far as its barracks have room, each soldier a villager who walks to a barracks with a free place and comes out a soldier (`balance` in `sim/population.ts`). **With no room in a barracks nobody is trained**, and there are no militia in peace. **Militia come only from a siege**: while the capital is besieged, every newcomer comes out of its gate a militiaman (twice as fast as usual, with a quarter more hit points and blows) and goes straight into the fight for it (`guardStep` in `sim/war.ts`). Once the siege is over, the militia take any free places in the barracks and train as soldiers; at war, those left stay round the castle and fight the next siege; in peace they go back to work. With the lock on the slider, the trades too leave their work to train, while the barracks have room.

**Houses look different from one to the next**: each keeps its tier's design (a hovel in one of three shapes, a stone house or manor facing either way), in one of **three colourings** picked at random when it is built: mud, whitewashed or timber walls for a hovel, and grey stone, warm limestone or red brick for the stone ones (`pVar`, `drawHouse` in `render/sprites.ts`).

**Trades, and the soldier slider.** A builder, a smith, a doctor, a scholar or a carpenter is a villager who took a trade; the kingdom card shows **Villagers** (those with none) and **Trades ▸**, which opens the list of who does what (`jobs` in `sim/population.ts`). The **People serving as soldiers** slider is held back by the trades: **the blue part of the bar on its right is the share of your people who have one**, and the slider stops where it starts, so recruiting only takes villagers with no trade and never stops a workshop (100 people, 10 of them with trades: the slider goes to 90%). The **🔒** by its label overrides that in an emergency (`draftAll`): once the free villagers are used up, the trades are called up too, at once, as **militia**, the reserve unit (they rest in houses and are called to the front like the rest), and their workshops take other villagers. Click it again and the limit slides back to what the trades alive allow. The kingdom card also counts as homeless only those who have been without a home for 10 seconds and are not off to build one.

**Auto-settle** (`ui/autosettle.ts`). A button on the kingdom card (top left): while it is on, every 5 seconds the kingdom settles land with **25% of its gold**, as if you had clicked and chosen 25%, so the early game needs no clicking. It picks the unclaimed tile beside your land nearest your castle, skips a round if 25% is under 2 gold, and **switches itself off** when no unclaimed land touches yours any more (and when a new match starts).

**The title screen** (`ui/title.ts`) is just the logo and the start controls (country, map, start) over a slideshow of **ten scenes from the game** (`src/assets/wall/`, captured from real matches: capitals, towns, a harbour, barracks, wars, wizards, Europe), one every 5 seconds with a slow cross-fade and drift. It runs only before a match. **Building texts** are one short line each (`info` in `sim/buildings.ts`).

**Land rush.** For the **first two minutes** of a match the land tax is **doubled** and settling unclaimed land costs **half** (`RUSH_TICKS`, `RUSH_COST`; for every kingdom, bots too). A gold note by the event log counts it down, and the log says when it is over. **A spawned villager takes 15 seconds** to put up his own home (`BUILD_TICKS`; a carpenter's extra hovels take 20 and cost nothing), but only while the kingdom has more people than beds and homes going up: with room to spare he goes straight to rest. **Carpenters and builders walk back to their workshop** for materials after each finished job (`TASK_SUPPLY`), so the further they worked from it the longer the wait. **The home-bonus line** in the kingdom card names *empty homes* and *home capacity* separately. **A crisis from a kingdom with no border** with you (a landing by sea) says it comes by sea instead of "on your border", and its balance of power reads *unknown*. **Militia are wounded like any soldier** (field medics); only plain villagers are not.

**Barracks on occupied ground do not work** (`barracksOf`, `standing` in `sim/army.ts`): one the enemy occupies raises no soldiers, trains none and is no army's home. Its soldiers are stationed in the next barracks with room, or serve on as militia; new soldiers come out of the next free barracks, or the castle; villagers called up train at another barracks, or at the castle as militia. **Every blow that lands is -15%, 0 or +15% of its damage** (`STRIKE_SWING`), at random, for a little chance in each duel. **When a kingdom falls to several attackers its land is shared by their war scores** (hidden: 1 point for each man of its they killed, half a point for each man of theirs it killed; `warScore`, `spoils` in `sim/war.ts`): each gets its share of the tiles, the ground it occupied first and then what lies nearest its own castle, whoever broke the castle; with one attacker it is all his.

**Barracks and armies** (`sim/army.ts`). Barracks side by side join into one barracks. Each module houses an army of 5 and has 5 armour racks, so four modules together keep one army of 20. Each barracks' army waits in front of it, and over its roof a label shows the army: the army's living soldiers (at home or away) over its room, then its soldiers by tier, for example `4/5 · 3 I · 1 II`. **The barracks' room is all the trained soldiers a kingdom can have.** Ask for more people under arms than that (the soldier slider counts everyone under arms) and the rest train at the castle as **militia**: with room for 15 and 20 called for, 15 walk to the barracks and 5 to the castle. Militia wear homespun with a sash in the kingdom's colour and carry a pitchfork; they are weaker than soldiers (5 hit points, 0.75 a blow, no armour), pay no tax and draw no wages. At peace they rest in their houses like villagers (those with no house patrol the city), they come out to defend the kingdom when it is at war; they don't march with the armies, but the war screen can send them too: under the armies, **Use militia** opens a slider for how many of those at home go (all of them to start with; the nearest the castle first). They go as a band of their own, paid a soldier's wage while out: to the border with an attack. With no barracks, they are all a kingdom can send. They go to war only when **called up as reserves** (below). Soldiers whose barracks burns, or shrinks under them, serve on as militia. A free place in a barracks (a soldier fell, or a new barracks went up) goes to **a militiaman first**: the nearest militia walk over together, one for each free place (a new barracks fills at once), train, and come out soldiers, while the reserves are not called up; with no militia at home, to the next new recruit. Only barracks armies go to war, and each barracks sends its own army, mustering in front of it and marching on its own. Two barracks in different places make two separate armies of 5, so it pays to build barracks beside each other for one big army, and keep a smaller one elsewhere. Click a barracks to see its army: how many are there and away, its soldiers by tier, the armour they wear and the armour waiting on the racks, and what the blacksmith is doing for it.

**Soldier tiers.** Every soldier starts at tier 1, archaic: bare-headed, in a tunic of the kingdom's colour, with a wooden spear tipped with flint (16 hit points, 1.5 damage a blow). Tier 2 is reinforced, in mail and a crested helmet (20 hit points, 2.25 damage). Tier 3 is heavy armour, in plate with a closed, plumed helm and a kite shield in the kingdom's colours (30 hit points, 3 damage). Armour comes from the **blacksmith**: its smith (a villager it takes on, replaced 5 seconds after he falls) waits until infantry research allows it, then fetches 5 copper from the castle, forges for 5 seconds, and carries the piece to the barracks nearest his smithy that still wants armour. A soldier of that barracks who comes by puts on the best piece on its racks that beats what he wears (his hit points rise with it), and his old armour is thrown away. A barracks gets reinforced armour until every rack has some, and only then heavy armour, which replaces it. A soldier who goes back to work leaves his armour on the racks.

Villagers don't gather (nothing lies on the map: see Resources); they build houses, and otherwise rest at home. Soldiers stand in their army's phalanx beside their barracks until it is sent to war; militia rest at home and stay there (wars are fought by the armies). People only walk inside their own borders (armies in the field aside), go around the castle rather than over it, and die if another kingdom takes the ground they stand on, or they fall in battle (soldiers have 8 to 15 hit points by tier, villagers 4; see below).

## Homes

Every person, villager or soldier, needs a home: a hovel holds one, a stone house two, a manor three (below). Whenever the kingdom has less room in its houses (counting ones being built) than people, a villager's next job is to build one, so a newcomer usually starts by building his own hovel before he goes to work. He picks the plot himself the way towns grow: beside other buildings and roads, outward from the edge of town, near the capital. He walks there carrying a plank, puts up a frame, works 4 seconds, and the hovel stands; new houses connect to the road network. If a house is already free (people died but their homes survived), a newcomer goes straight to work.

With more room than people for 20 seconds, the spare houses stand empty and run down, the farthest from the capital first; as people arrive again, the innermost empty houses are lived in first (`sim/population.ts`). Land caps housing: once a kingdom's plots are full, new people stay homeless until it expands.

**Resting at home.** A villager with a home and nothing to build or do doesn't wander the streets: he walks to the nearest house with a free bed and goes in, out of sight. **Smoke rises only from houses with someone resting inside**, so a smoking chimney means a pair of hands is at home. Resting, he checks every 10 to 12 seconds whether houses need building for the homeless, and the homeless, who have nowhere to rest, look as often (`goRest` in `sim/population.ts`). **The way round the kingdom.** Straight lines (and a detour round the castle) are all a villager's route knew, and in a kingdom with bays, coves and borders the straight way home from a tree is often shut, which left villagers idle with a whole forest in the kingdom. So each kingdom has a **walking field**: the steps from its castle's gate over its own free land, worked out again every 10 seconds for everyone at once (`walkOf`, `fillWalk` in `sim/population.ts`). What the field doesn't reach isn't looked for, and what it reaches is walked to down the field and out again, pulled straight where the line is clear (`routeField`). Builders, smiths, resting villagers and soldiers walking home use the field when the straight way is shut; a house whose door the field doesn't reach is no home to go to. Enemies at war who find a house with people resting inside go for it (attackers along the front): when they break it open, the people come out of the rubble beside it and fight (`evict` in `sim/war.ts`). On a 60-kingdom Europe at minute 15, about half the people rest at home (the rest are gathering), and a tick of the simulation takes 10 to 12 ms instead of 18.

The people who have lived in the kingdom longest get the houses, so the newest arrivals are the homeless ones, and whoever has been homeless longest moves in first when a house comes free. Homeless villagers pay no tax. A villager homeless for a minute turns **beggar**, but only when the kingdom is too crammed to build in: one on his way to build himself a house, or building it, and one between jobs while a plot is free for a house, is never turned beggar, however far he has to walk (`roomForHome` in `sim/population.ts`). **Villagers never leave their own land**: a step onto another kingdom's ground is refused and the walk planned again, and one found on such ground in peace (the border moved under him) is set down on his own land rather than lost; only conquest in a war kills the people on ground it takes. he crawls slowly about town in rags, does no work, pays no tax and can't be recruited, until a home comes free for him and he goes back to work. **Half a minute later (a minute and a half without a roof) he dies of want**, dropping where he stands like the fallen of a battle, but without a wound or a pool of blood (`STARVE_TICKS` in `sim/population.ts`); the log tells you when your own begin to die. Homeless soldiers stay soldiers.

## Buildings

Houses are built by villagers (above). Everything else is placed by the kingdom from the four building menus on the right-hand buttons (B reopens the last one) and paid for from its stockpile: **urban development** (farm, builders guild, carpenters, coin minter, hospital, and the region forts of watchtowers), **military** (every kind of barracks: barracks and archer barracks; the blacksmith and the naval base), **resource buildings** (plantation, stone quarry, copper mine) and **society advancement** (the research station, for now). There are no zoom buttons (the mouse wheel and pinch zoom) and no centre button (Space centres on your capital):

**Prices round to the nearest whole good**: each building of a kind you own makes the next a few percent dearer (the table below), but a small price such as a watchtower's 2 wood does not jump to 3 at the first one you build; the first of any kind costs exactly its base price.

**Building over buildings.** You can place a building over your own buildings: whatever stands under it (houses, workshops, towers, a frame going up) is pulled down when it goes up (the placing banner says so), and you pay only for the new one. Only the castle and buildings on fire can't be built over. Bots do the same with houses that stand where they want a wall tower or a workshop (`Over`, `demolish` in `sim/buildings.ts`); the people who lived there build anew when they run short of room. **Every plot of land can be built on**, mountains and snowfields too: the high ground isn't wasted space any more. Buildings run by themselves; only the builders guild and the blacksmith need a worker. They are modular: a plot draws itself from its four neighbours, so fields of farms merge into one farmland with a single fence round the outside, barracks and minters next to each other become one long hall, and neighbouring watchtowers are joined by walls (`render/sprites.ts`). **The walls are real**: while both towers stand, nobody but their kingdom and its allies can cross one (`buildWalls` in `sim/defense.ts`). Armies go round a wall if they can; walled out completely, they knock down a tower holding it up, and the wall falls with it. A ring of towers makes a city wall. **Resource gathering.** Plantations, quarries and copper mines are placed one plot each, and two side by side stay two buildings until *resource gathering* research reaches tier 2: then every pair of single ones of a kind side by side joins into one 2×1 building, at once and whenever another is placed. At tier 3, any four of a kind filling a 2×2 square (singles, a pair and two singles, two pairs) join into one 2×2 building. A joined building makes far more than its parts did apart:

| Every 30 s | 1 plot (tier 1) | 2×1 (tier 2) | 2×2 (tier 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plantation | 2 wood | 5 wood | 15 wood |
| Stone quarry | 2 stone | 5 stone | 15 stone |
| Copper mine | 1 copper | 3 copper | 10 copper |

A joined building is one building: the top-left plot holds it and the rest point to it, and if any of its plots is taken, the whole of it burns. One standing on ground the enemy holds makes nothing.

**How much buildings take.** Before they burn: a hovel 20 blows (a stone house 60, a manor 120; a blow on a building is 1.5), a plantation, quarry or copper mine 30 per tier, a farm, coin minter, research station, builders guild, carpenters or blacksmith 30, a barracks 40, watchtowers and the castle by tier (below). Bots build with simple priorities of their own (`sim/bots.ts`).

## Research, better homes and armour

Each research station earns 1 research point a second, and all of them go to the one topic the kingdom is studying: **housing**, **infantry**, **watchtowers**, the **castle** or **taxation** (`sim/research.ts`). Clicking a station opens the research panel: a button per topic with its tier, and a progress bar below; the lit one is being studied, and clicking the other switches (progress on both is kept). Housing is studied first, from the moment the first station stands. Every topic starts at tier 1 and goes up to tier 3, and costs the same everywhere: **400 points** for tier 2 and **800** more for tier 3 (field medics 400, 800 and 1200; `TIER_XP` in `sim/research.ts`). Each station employs a **scholar**, drawn as a **wizard** (pointed hat, purple robe, white beard, a staff with a glowing orb), a villager who works there as a profession (counted with the other trades, and held back from recruiting by the slider's blue bar). **A station earns its points only while its scholar stands at its door** (`studying` in `sim/population.ts`). Two stations side by side are drawn as one long model (a hall between two round towers); they are still two stations. A finished topic gives way to the next. What is learned is kept for good, even if every station burns.

- **Infantry** lets the blacksmith forge reinforced armour (tier 2) and then heavy armour (tier 3) for the barracks (above).
- **Watchtowers** lets the builders rebuild each watchtower a tier up, and the **castle** rebuilds the castle at once, free (`sim/defense.ts`):

  | | Shoots every | Damage | Blows to break |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Watchtower, tier 1 / 2 / 3 | 4 / 3.5 / 3 s | 3 / 4.5 / 6 | 40 / 60 / 80 |
  | Castle, tier 1 / 2 / 3 | 2 / 1.75 / 1.5 s (like two towers) | 3 / 4.5 / 6 | 400 / 800 / 1200 |

  The castle mends itself, 2 blows a second; towers don't. A tower is rebuilt taller with a timber gallery and a pointed roof at tier 2, and as a pale keep with a spire, banners and braziers at tier 3; the castle becomes a fortress, its towers roofed and a turret on the keep, then a citadel trimmed in gold. A tower looks battered from half its blows; the castle looks damaged from a third and badly damaged from two thirds, with breaches, soot and fires.
- **Taxation** raises each villager's tax by 0.5 gold a second per tier: 0.1, 0.6, 1.1 (`taxRate` in `sim/sim.ts`).
- **Housing** lets the builders guild rebuild houses, and **watchtowers** lets it rebuild watchtowers a tier up, for nothing (it takes time, below). The hovels villagers put up are tier 1 and they keep building hovels; at tier 2 the guild can turn them into **stone houses** (room for 2, three times the blows to burn), and at tier 3 into **manors** (room for 3, six times a hovel's).
- **Resource gathering** lets plantations, quarries and copper mines join (above).

**A capital on a neck of land.** When the sea runs close on both sides of the castle (Greece's on the small Europe map), its walls cut the gate and the rest of the kingdom apart, and the smith, the builders and the villagers who spawn at the gate had no way to the town. The walking field now goes **through the castle**, by its back gate, but only to reach ground it cannot reach round it (`fillWalk` in `sim/population.ts`).

A builder (or a smith) **passes over what he cannot walk to** (across the water, behind the enemy's lines) and goes on to the next, and the click message says so when nothing is within reach. A **builders guild** takes the villager nearest it as its builder; he wears a leather cap.

**The builders guild is the city investment.** There is no investment slider and no ledger of goods any more: the guild costs **10 wood, 50 stone and 10 copper** (each guild you own makes the next 10% dearer), and what its builder does costs nothing. When research allows, he walks from building to building and rebuilds each one a tier, working non-stop with his hammer: **20 seconds** to raise a tier 1 house or watchtower to tier 2 and **40 seconds** from tier 2 to tier 3 (`FIX_SECONDS` in `sim/population.ts`). Each time he is free he takes whichever job costs the **least time, walk included** (`goImprove`): the lowest buildings first, since 20 seconds of work beats 40; a tier 2 one only when the nearest tier 1 is so far off that walking to it costs more than the time it saves, or when only tier 2 ones are left. Watchtowers and houses are one pool, so a tier 2 tower beside him is raised rather than a hovel a long walk away. In the end every house is a manor (with housing tier 3) and every tower tier 3. An improved building is as good as new. The builder runs or fights when enemies come like any villager; if he falls, another villager takes up his tools 5 seconds later. Several guilds work side by side. Bots build stations once they have found their feet, study whichever topic is behind, and build guilds once they can use them.

## Regions

**The land is divided into regions** (`sim/regions.ts`), drawn on the map as thin dark lines. On the real maps they are the **real provinces**: Natural Earth's first-level divisions (public domain), merged and split by `tools/regions.ts` to a balanced size, so a small country's dozens of districts gather into a few regions (Italy's provinces into Toscana, Lombardia, ...) and a vast Russian oblast is cut into compact pieces named by their compass point ("Northern Arkhangelsk"). Desert counts for less, so the Sahara makes bigger regions. **Europe has 589 regions**, the Balkans 100, the world 248; islands too small to be a region belong to none. On a generated map the regions are grown at random over the land, about 1,100 tiles each, with made-up names. The grids are stored run-length encoded in `sim/regiondata.ts` (run `node tools/regions.ts <natural-earth folder>` to draw them again).

**A region goes whole to the kingdom that owns more than half of its land: 51%** (`CONTROL`; it was 60%, which left a region split 55% to 45% with nobody's flag). The other kingdoms' ground in it is **pushed out**: it becomes the controller's, and their buildings on it burn. Its **flag** goes up on the tile furthest from its edge, in the controller's colours, and nobody else can settle any of it. A region with another kingdom's castle in it can't be taken from that kingdom this way. Settling is still tile by tile: a region with no flag is open to anyone.

## Diplomacy and war

**Single watchtowers are gone**: watchtowers are built only as region forts (*Forts*, below), and the castle doesn't shoot. Naval invasions are gone too: wars are fought on land, region by region. Ships, naval bases and colonies by sea are as before.

Clicking another kingdom's land opens diplomacy (`ui/diplomacy.ts`, `sim/war.ts`): **Propose alliance** (allies never go to war with each other) or **Claim regions…**.

**Claims, the crisis.** *Claim regions…* closes the panel and lets you pick regions of theirs on the map: the **first must border a region you control**, each next one **must border one already picked**, at most **four**, and only regions they control (with their flag up) can be claimed. The regions you could pick next are lit in faint gold, those picked in red; click a picked one to let it (and those after it) go, then **Claim**. That is a **crisis**: they answer within 30 seconds (bots within 4). They **give the regions up** (they are yours at once, with no blood shed: bots do this only against a kingdom far stronger than they are), or they **refuse, and it is war**; silence is a refusal. Claims made on you show in the war bar at the top with **Give up** and **Refuse · war**; your own with **Take back**.

**Stationing.** In peace or war, an army can be sent to sit in **any region of its kingdom's** (or an ally's, or at war one it occupies): select it and right-click the region. It marches there and holds it (paid the field wage, 0.3); **🏠 Home** garrisons it at its barracks again, for less upkeep (0.22), where its men heal. Armies are meant to wait in the border regions, for a quick answer to a war: one stationed beside a region under attack marches in to defend it (the second wave, below). Bots station the armies they have beyond the first in their regions on the border with their strongest neighbour.

**At war nothing moves on its own.** Each **army** is a **phalanx**: the soldiers of a barracks (barracks side by side join into one barracks, 5 men a module), or of an order group of barracks (merged armies, the **⛓** button of the Armies list, at home only), standing in **ranks and files**, wide and shallow. At home it stands beside its barracks. Take one in hand (its men are ringed in gold) by clicking a soldier of it on the map or its row in the **Armies** list, then **right-click a region**:
- a region you claimed of the enemy, **beside ground you hold**: the army **attacks** it. It marches to the region's flag (below) and holds it;
- one of **your regions** (or an ally's): the army is **stationed** there; in a region the enemy claimed, it defends it.

Regions that were not claimed can't be attacked, and the attacker can't go deeper than the ground it holds: each region attacked must border it. Two or more border regions can be attacked at once, by different armies. **One army per region and side.** Under an army's row: **↩ Retreat** (break off the fight and fall back to the nearest region your kingdom holds) and **🏠 Home** (back to the barracks, where its men heal fully in 30 seconds and are paid the idle wage, 0.22 instead of 0.3, or 0.4 attacking or fighting). An army retreating or going home is let alone for 40 seconds.

**The march** (`sim/roads.ts`). No roads are laid or drawn any more. An army's way is found in two steps: first the **chain of regions** it passes through, flag to flag (a search over the region map, a third kingdom's regions dearer), then **one search over the tiles** of those regions and the regions touching them (hills and woods dearer, mountains much dearer), from where it stands to where it is going. The way is then **pulled straight**: every bend that a straight line can skip, over ground no rougher and never onto water, is cut out (looking up to 160 tiles ahead), so a column walks smooth lines and no longer zigzags back and forth on the way between regions. **Water is never a wall**: it is dear to cross (7 times open ground), so armies go round a bay where that is not much longer and **row across** a strait or a sea where it must be, each man in a small boat; a way the regions' search can't find is looked for in a box round both ends, and then on a coarse grid of 4×4 tiles over the whole map, which always finds one. On the march an army is a **column at most 10 men across** (`MARCH_FILES`), the rest in ranks behind, each rank following the trail its head has left, so the column bends along its way instead of cutting corners over the water. It marches at 6 pixels a second on open ground (slower in rough country and on the water), waits a little for stragglers but never stops for them, and **a column that gets no nearer its next waypoint for 12 seconds looks for its way again** from where it stands. Its men go round a castle's walls by the nearer corner. Standing or fighting, it forms up in **rows of at most 10 men** (the combat width, `WIDTH`; below).

**Archers** (`sim/army.ts`, `volley` in `sim/war.ts`). A soldier takes a spearman's or an archer's place in his army as he joins it, whichever it is shorter of. An archer has **half a spearman's hit points and blows** (8 and 0.75), **wears no armour** (the racks of an army arm its spearmen only), and is paid **a tenth more** (0.33 a second in the field, `ARCHER_WAGE`). **They stand in the last line** of the formation, an empty row behind the spearmen (the battle line, below), **shoot in the fire phases of a battle** (an arrow a tick each, 1 damage; a small arrow: a thin dark shaft with a pale head on a low arc, much smaller than a watchtower's bolt), and fight hand to hand, badly, only when the enemy reaches their row. Archers shoot only in a battle (and at a castle they storm). When the spearmen are nearly gone, **↩ Retreat** saves the archers.

**Morale.** An army that retreats **rallies for 30 seconds** (`RALLY_TICKS`): it takes no orders until then (its row says how long), so a retreat is a commitment.

**Standby.** Ordering an army to a region where another army of yours is **fighting** doesn't send it: it **stands by** where it is (`next`, `standby` in `sim/war.ts`), and **marches in with its order the moment the first army is beaten or falls back**; if the first wins, it stands down. The defender queues the next army for a region it defends the same way. Less waiting for a battle to end, and less micromanaging.

**Capitals: the siege.** A kingdom's capital region is special. **It has no flag**: the castle stands for it, and it is taken only by **storming the castle** (`siteOf`, `besieging`, `siegeStep`, `castleFalls` in `sim/war.ts`). An army attacking it marches to the castle's gate; standing there with no battle on, its spearmen spread round the walls and **strike them all at once** (the castle is the one enemy many men fight together, not one on one), and its archers stand back and shoot the castle's defenders, or else the walls. The castle takes 400, 800 or 1,200 blows by its tier, **mends 2 a second**, and **shoots like a fort of tier 4** at the attackers in its region (`CAPITAL_TIER`, `capitalIn`, `fortTier` in `sim/buildings.ts`): every 3 seconds a random enemy soldier there takes **16** (the forts' 2, 4, 8 doubled again; its arrows don't fly beyond its region). Its owner's armies are sent to defend it like any region (they stand before its gate), and fight the besiegers in the field first. While it is besieged the kingdom's newcomers come out as militia, twice as fast, to fight (above). A bar over the keep shows how much of it still stands. **When its blows are spent the castle falls, and the kingdom with it**, to the besieger: a last stand, with every advantage to the defender. It costs no upkeep and needs no fort built (none can be). **A capital's region can't be surrendered**: a crisis that claims it can't be answered by giving up (bots refuse it, the player's *Give up* is greyed out), and neither giving the war goals up nor any peace hands it over.

**Barracks are lost with their region.** When a region is occupied, the barracks of its owner there **burn** (`razeBarracks` in `sim/war.ts`); ground ceded in a peace hands over its buildings but not its barracks, which burn too, and so do a fallen kingdom's. A kingdom's army is only what its own barracks raise: no sudden army from conquests.

**Holding what is taken.** An attacker can station an army in a region **it occupies** (right-click it): the army keeps it, since the owner can't raise its flag while an enemy army stands there.

**Reinforce.** New soldiers of an army's barracks, and its wounded healed in the hospital, **wait at the barracks** while the army is away. The **⇪ Reinforce · n** button under an army's row (n is how many wait) sends them after it: they **ride** (each on a horse, drawn under him, which is why they go faster than the army marched: 1.8 times a man's pace) together along one way to wherever the army is, following it if it moves, and **join it as its rearmost ranks** once they reach it. If they reach it in the middle of a battle they **join the back of the phalanx**, and step up into the front as the men before them fall. An army fighting with everyone down but reinforcements on the way is not yet beaten. Bots reinforce their armies at war when enough men wait.

**An army is at most 100 men** (`MAX_ARMY` in `sim/army.ts`): barracks side by side beyond 20 modules make a second army, and merged armies can't go past 100 together.

**The flag.** An invader's army **sitting** in a claimed region (anywhere in it, holding) with no army of its owner's there **lowers the flag**: **20 seconds** on the clock from up to down, whatever the army's size (`CAPTURE_TICKS`). When it is all the way down the region is **occupied**: no flag, its ground hatched in the occupier's colour, paying its owner no tax; the next claimed region beside it may now be attacked. **While two armies fight in a region its flag doesn't move.** An army of its owner's sitting there alone (the defender who won the fight, or one sent to take it back) **raises it again at the same rate**; an occupied region is won back when its flag is all the way up. A flag left half down creeps back up when nobody is there.

**The second wave.** The defender may put one army in each claimed region; when an enemy army goes for one of them with nobody of the defender's there, a defending army holding a claimed region beside it marches in (`secondWave`), so armies placed behind the first act as the reserve.

**The battle line** (`tidyRows`, `slotOf` in `sim/war.ts`). An army forms up in **rows of at most 10 men** (the combat width), in **priority lines** from the front: **line 1, the spearmen**, as many rows as they fill; lines 2 and 3 are kept for units to come, and **one empty row** stands for them; **line 4, the archers**; line 5 is a capital's castle alone, the last thing fought (the siege, above). So 20 spearmen and 5 archers stand as 10, 10, an empty row, 5; and 10 spearmen with 15 archers as 10, an empty row, 10, 5. Rows are kept full from the back: a fallen man's place is taken by the nearest man of the last row of his kind, and the last row closes up; newcomers and reinforcements join the last row.

**Battles** (`engage`, `fight`). Two hostile armies that come within 26 pixels of each other fight. Each army's front row is its **fighting row**. Spearmen close with the enemy: the army that was holding its ground stays and the other comes up to it. **A row fights until every man of it is down**: its fallen leave gaps, nobody steps into them, and only when the last man falls does **the next row move up into the fighting zone**. In the fighting rows each man fights **the man opposite him**; a man with nobody opposite goes for the nearest enemy of the fighting row with room about him (**three on one at most**: from the front and from each side), so the last men of a broken row are overwhelmed quickly and the whole line stays busy; the rows behind wait their turn. **When an army's spearmen are all down its archers hold their ground** and the enemy's fighting row comes to them: archers with the enemy upon them **fight hand to hand** (at half a spearman's blow) **and stop shooting**, while the archers' rows behind them shoot on. **Two armies with only archers left stay where they are and shoot** at each other; nobody closes. Blows in a battle line land three times as hard as elsewhere (`BATTLE_DMG`). The army with nobody left standing in the field has lost; its men at home are its depot.

**Fire and melee, and the dice.** As in Europa Universalis a battle goes by **phases of three ticks**, a tick a second on the clock (`BATTLE_TICK`, `PHASE_TICKS`): it begins with **fire**, then **melee**, then fire again, and so on. As each phase begins **each side rolls a die** for it: **1** is **-20%** to its arrows or blows of that phase, 2 -10%, **3** even, 4 +6.7%, 5 +13.3%, **6** **+20%** (`diceFactor`). **In a fire phase** every archer who is not in the melee looses **one arrow a tick** (1 damage, by the die) at a man of the enemy army picked at random, its front rows likelier; nobody strikes, but the lines close and men take their places. **In a melee phase** every man of the two fighting rows strikes **once a tick** (each at his own beat of the second, so the line is alive rather than striking as one), and no arrows fly. Over each line a plate shows the phase (**a bow** for fire, **crossed swords** for melee), three pips for its ticks, the die, and what it is worth (*Fire +13%*).

| 20 reinforced (II) + 10 archers against 40 archaic spearmen | the armoured army, 15 left, every time | about 38 s |

What they show: a row of ten takes about 15 seconds to break, so a battle lasts as long as its rows are deep; **archers behind enough spearmen decide battles** (every archer shoots in every fire phase, while only ten spearmen can fight at once), and archers the enemy reaches die fast; armour is worth about two men for one. A battle costs next to nothing to work out: 0.1 to 0.2 ms a tick for the whole game with 200 men fighting, and a 24-kingdom game on Europe still averages about 3.4 ms a tick.

**War goals, both sides.** Once a crisis is refused, **either side** may set its war goals at will (**✎ War goals** in the war bar; `setClaims`, `claimsProblem`): claim **any** region of the other's, or let claims go. The attacker that began it (`began`) and the **defender** alike: a defender who claims regions of the attacker's can **attack them** with its armies like any attacker, whether or not the attacker holds regions of its own (each region attacked must still border ground it holds). The war needs a claim on one side at least: a side may let all its own claims go only while the other claims something. A region let go that its claimant occupied is free again. The war bar lists **your goals** and **theirs**, each with how many are taken.

**Ending a war.** A side that occupies **every** region it claims has won: the war ends, those regions are its, and the other side keeps the claimed regions it holds. Otherwise:
- **Demand war goals** (a side with claims): if the other accepts, **each side gets the claimed regions it holds now**, taken ones only (`makePeace` with `goals`);
- **Give war goals** (a side the other claims of): peace at once, the other gets **every** region it claims, taken or not, and the giver keeps the claimed regions of the other's it holds;
- **white peace**: everything as it was before the war, every occupied region free again, nothing changes hands.
An offer stands 30 seconds; changing the claims calls offers made off; a demand for war goals made of you can be accepted from the war bar. **A kingdom whose castle region is given up falls**: its land, people and buildings go to the winner.

**Bots** (`botWar`, `counter` in `sim/bots.ts`) lay claims on the weakest neighbour they have a region beside (one to four regions by temper and odds), answer claims on them (they give up only to a kingdom far stronger), send each free army to the nearest claimed region they can attack, put one army in each region claimed of them (those under attack first), bring armies losing badly out of a fight and worn-down ones home, and station spare armies on the border in peace. Attacking, they demand their war goals once they hold most of them, offer white peace when losing, and claim more when winning. **Defending, a bot whose defence holds** (it is winning, the attacker holds under half its goals, and the war is 30 seconds old) **strikes back**: it claims a region of the attacker's on its border, sends its armies (those holding its regions too, while no army of the attacker's is in the field) to take it, and once it is taken claims the next, **one after another, until it holds as many as its temper wants (1 to 3)**; then it **demands its war goals** for peace. A defender takes white peace when it holds none of its own goals and offers it when losing for a while. **It gives its war goals up only once the attacker holds three in four of them** and the war goes against it (`yields`); losing badly before that, it offers peace on what each side holds, so one lost battle never hands over a chain of regions. A demand for war goals is accepted by a bot losing badly, or one that gets at least as much as it gives.

**Performance.** The region war is cheap: an army is one formation (a march is one path for the whole phalanx, its men walk to their places in it), the flags are counted only for the regions where an army stands, and the per-nation tile counts of each region are kept up as tiles change hands. A 24-kingdom bot game on Europe averages about 4 ms a tick over its first 13 minutes (16,000 ticks; the game needs 50 ms to keep real time); the standard 24-kingdom match on the small Europe the game plays averages about 3 ms, and on Emberland about 1.7 ms, with a rare long tick (a kingdom annexed, a long way across the sea worked out) up to about 0.4 s. A march's way is a single search over the few regions it crosses, a few milliseconds.

**Field medics, the hospital and the wounded** (`sim/medics.ts`). *Field medics* is a research topic of three steps: **400 points** to learn it, **800** more for a second tier and **1200** more for a third (the panel counts them as tier 1 to 3; `TIER_XP` in `sim/research.ts`). With it learned, **8%** of the soldiers who fall are only **wounded**, **12%** with the second tier and **20%** with the third (`woundChance`): they lie where they fell like the dead, but with no blood and a **cross** over them, **grey** while nobody is coming. A wounded man **bleeds out in 45 seconds**; when a doctor is on his way the cross turns **green** and the clock stops. The **hospital** (urban development, 15 wood, 25 stone, 10 copper, 2 plots, grayed out until the research is done; a medieval infirmary with a red cross and two beds in its open wards) takes **two villagers as doctors**, who wait inside. When a soldier of the kingdom lies wounded on the hospital's landmass, the nearest doctor **runs out to him, 50% faster than usual**, picks him up, **carries him to the hospital at the same speed** and tends him in one of the **two beds** (a soldier is drawn lying in it) for **7 seconds**; the doctor stays with him until then, and goes out again afterwards. The soldier then stands up at the door and **goes back to his barracks**, where he waits with the new soldiers to be sent to his army with **⇪ Reinforce** (above). The point is to cut the permanent losses of a war. Bots learn field medics once they have an army and gold to spare, and build hospitals as their army and land grow (`wishlist` in `sim/bots.ts`).

**Fighting.** Soldiers have 16, 20 or 30 hit points by tier and villagers 8 (everything has twice the hit points it had, and every blow, shot and blow on a building a half more, so fights are longer and the higher tiers are worth more). A soldier strikes once a second, hitting 70% of the time for 1.5, 2.25 or 3 by tier; a militiaman hits for 1.125 (10 hit points) and a villager for 0.75. **Fights are duels**: when two men fight, each is the other's only opponent, and nobody else may strike either of them (`partner`, `locked` in `sim/war.ts`). A man who finds his enemy already fighting somebody picks another, or waits in his place in the line, and the next man takes a fallen man's place only once he is dead; a man who runs more than a few pixels from his duel is free of it. So an overwhelming army can't swarm the few who stand against it: it fights them one on one, and the war is slower. Men in a duel don't push the front forward before them (the line closes over the gap when it ends), and a villager still runs or fights at the sight of any enemy. Watchtowers shoot only as region forts (above): a random enemy soldier in the region every 3 seconds. A fighter lunges at his enemy as he strikes; whoever is hit flashes white and staggers back. Everyone heals 0.1 a second. Wounds show: soldiers look hurt (blood, a dented helmet, torn mail) below two thirds of their hit points and near death below a third, head sunk, leaving a short trail of blood; villagers look bloodied below 60%. The fallen drop where they stood, in a spreading pool of blood, and fade after half a minute.

**Story events** (`sim/story.ts`, told in the message log by `ui/story.ts`) are moments in a kingdom's life that change what its people do. Each has a check, run every so often for every kingdom, and a cooldown. The first: **the mob**. When enemy soldiers reach the castle, or burn a crowded part of town, the villagers nearby drop everything and swarm them, whatever they would have chosen alone.

**The sea** (`sim/naval.ts`). **Ships keep to the water**: a course is pulled straight only where a ship's hull (a margin round the line) clears the land, and a ship recalled goes home back over the waypoints it passed, never straight across to the ones it had not reached. A **naval base** (military menu, 15 wood and 5 stone) is a harbour of **8 plots**, 4×2 or turned 2×4 to fit, **a third of them at least on the water**; any land in it must be your own and some of it must be land. It is drawn with a plank quay along the shore, piers running out, a warehouse, a crane. Click it for the **ship yard**: *Build a ship · 5 wood* takes 15 seconds on the clock (`SHIP_TICKS`), a slider shows the ship in the yard, and the panel counts ships ready, being built and at sea. The fleet belongs to the kingdom, not to a base: every base may send any of it. Ships sail only on water, routed over the sea from the base's dock (`seaNearest`, `seaPaths`), and sail home to dock after their work; a ship at sea doesn't count as ready. **Naval settlement**: the settle menu (click unclaimed land) ends in a single **⚓** button; with no naval base or no ship ready it is grayed out and says what is missing (*Build a naval base to settle by sea*, *You need a ship*), and land of an island where you have none can only be settled this way. Click it, then click the land to send the ship to (a green mark shows where it would land, red where it cannot): only then does the menu come back with the 25% to 100% buttons for how much of your gold the ship carries. On an island, a ship of the fleet sails from the nearest base to the coast nearest the click, at any distance; on land joined to your own, the click must be within 10 tiles of the shore (`COAST_REACH`), else *Too far from the coast*. The ship puts **one tile** ashore, the gold spreads the border **from that tile** like any settling (over its own landmass: `Attack.seed`), and the ship sails home and counts again when it docks. From then on the land is settled like the rest, and a plain settle spreads over mainland and island alike. A **sea lane** opens from the base to the landing, and villagers cross it **in small boats**, 4 times as fast as on foot, to found the new village and build houses there; they need no ship. Ships and invasions: see *War* above.

**Bots at sea** (`sim/botsea.ts`). By its temper and by how much room it has left, a bot looks to the sea: a kingdom with no unclaimed land at its border (an island kingdom like Britain, or one that has filled its mainland) wants it most, and builds a **naval base** once it has a town and farms, a few **ships** (enough for its biggest army and a colony), **colonies** on the nearest unclaimed land across the water, and, when it has nobody to attack by land, a **landing** on the weakest kingdom it has no land border with whose coast its ships can reach (the same odds its land attacks use). It gives the word for its landing itself once the crisis's 30 seconds are up, or calls it off if outmatched. A kingdom with room to spare, beggars to house or no army hardly looks at all.

## Resources

**There are no resources on the map.** No trees, rocks or ore lie about to be gathered, and no villager goes out to fetch anything: the whole stockpile of wood, stone and copper comes from the three gathering buildings a kingdom builds (below), and every villager stays in his house until something is wanted of him. (`sim/resources.ts` is just the three goods now; the map shows only the cacti of the deserts.) This keeps a big game light: nobody searches the land for work, nobody carries a load, and about two thirds of the people are out of sight and out of the simulation's way at any time.

**The first plantation and quarry a kingdom builds are free** (the copper mine costs its 10 wood and 10 stone) (`costFor` in `sim/buildings.ts`), so every kingdom can start its stockpile; the next ones cost what their cards say, **and each one a kingdom owns makes the next of its kind 10% of its base price dearer** (5% for a farm; a joined building counts for the plots it covers; `RISE`, `costFor` in `sim/buildings.ts`), so a kingdom spreads over kinds rather than piling up one. Each makes goods every 30 seconds by its tier, and with resource gathering research two side by side join (tier 2), then four in a square (tier 3) into one that makes much more than the parts:

| Building | Cost | Alone | Two joined | Four in a square |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plantation | 10 wood | +2 wood | +5 | +15 |
| Stone quarry | 5 wood, 10 stone | +2 stone | +5 | +15 |
| Copper mine | 10 wood, 10 stone | +1 copper | +3 | +10 |

**Bots plan for this** (`groundwork` in `sim/bots.ts`): before anything else they build plantations (a plantation pays for itself in two and a half minutes), then quarries and copper mines, and a research station; they save up for these (nothing else is bought with the goods they need), place each kind side by side and then in squares (`squareFill`), and study resource gathering as soon as they have two of them, to join them. They also build more farms than before, each 5% dearer than the last: farms are what make a kingdom grow, with more people and more taxes.

## The determinism test

`tests/determinism.test.ts` plays three scripted 5-minute matches on the simulation alone and compares fingerprints of the full game state (ownership, troops, buildings, roads, fires, attacks) every 20 seconds of game time against `tests/golden.json`. A pass means the game still plays exactly as it did when the file was recorded. It was first recorded from the original single-file version, and re-recorded when the map grew to 400×248 tiles, when gold replaced troops, when the ocean shrank, when war replaced buying enemy land (it now fingerprints the armies in the field too), when wars became lasting with occupation, when wild resources were halved, and when wars moved to fronts (and mountains became buildable, settling got cheaper on rough ground, and buildings could go over buildings), when wars began with a crisis and the militia came in, when beggars began to die of want, bots learned to end stalled wars, and fronts held their ground a tile closer to their men, and most recently when villagers began to rest at home, felled trees vanished and bots began saving a war chest. (The matches are on generated maps: Europe's redrawing didn't change them, nor did the armies' paths being worked out over the box round the kingdoms concerned, `sim/field.ts`, rather than the whole map.)

If you change gameplay on purpose, the test will fail. That's expected. Re-record the golden file with:

```sh
UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 npm test
```
