# Emberfront

A fast, arcade-paced browser territory war: settle open land with gold, then make alliances or send your soldiers by land or sea to conquer your neighbours, while every kingdom grows its own villages, farms, mines, towers and roads.

## Running it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # play at http://localhost:5173 with live reload
npm test           # simulation regression test (no browser needed)
npm run build      # type-check and build a static site into dist/
```

`dist/` can be hosted anywhere that serves static files (GitHub Pages, Netlify, itch.io). It uses relative paths, so it works from a subfolder.

## How the code is organised

```
src/
  core/      constants, colour/number helpers, seeded random & noise, countries and their flags
  sim/       the game itself: map generation, territory, buildings, people, research, war & diplomacy, the sea (naval.ts), bots
  render/    camera, terrain & territory pixels, building, ship, nature & people sprites, effects, frame drawing
  ui/        HUD, log & toasts, buildings & research panels, diplomacy & war chips, mouse/touch/keyboard input
  game.ts    connects the simulation to the screen (new game, spawn, player orders)
  main.ts    boot and the main loop
tests/       scripted matches checked against recorded results
tools/       realmaps.ts: draws the real-world maps from Natural Earth; dem.py: Europe's elevation for it
```

**The one rule: `src/sim` never touches the page.** No `document`, no canvas, no `Math.random`. The simulation:

- runs in fixed 0.1 s ticks and gets all of its randomness from the map seed, so the same seed plus the same player orders always produce the same match;
- takes player actions only as **commands** (`queueCommand`), which are applied at the start of the next tick and re-checked there;
- reports what happened through **events** (`sim/events.ts`: tile changed, building placed, capital fell, …). Rendering and UI subscribe to these; with no subscribers (a server, a test) the simulation runs the same, just without visuals.

That split is what makes online multiplayer possible later: every browser runs the same simulation and only commands go over the network.

**Nothing scans the whole map once a second any more.** Which kingdoms share a border (and how long it is) and the middle of each kingdom (where its label goes) are counted once at the start of a match (`scanContacts` in `sim/census.ts`) and then kept exact as each tile changes hands (`setOwner` in `sim/territory.ts`); a test checks they always match a fresh count. On Europe's 3.5 million tiles that alone saved a tenth of the simulation's time.

**Keeping the late game fast.** Measured on Europe with 63 rivals (a headless profile of 300 ticks at 2,000 to 40,000 ticks in), a tick costs 2.6 ms early, 24 ms at 17 minutes and 70 ms at 33 minutes; the game needs 50 ms to keep real time (20 ticks a second), and the cost per person triples as territories and wars grow. The drawing costs 3 to 4 ms a frame and doesn't grow. What was done about it, none of it changing how anything plays: **a front's line** (`lineOf` scanned the whole kingdom's box every 5 ticks for every front, the biggest single cost late on) is now kept for up to 20 ticks and worked out again at once whenever land is occupied or freed, orders change, or a strip is about to be taken (`LINE_EVERY`, `occSig`, `redeploy` in `sim/war.ts`); the tower-safe line of defending armies is kept 60 ticks; **the mob check** looks only at the soldiers of kingdoms at war with the one it checks instead of everyone on the map for every kingdom; `updateWar` makes two passes over the people instead of five, and healing is done a tenth of the men at a time (ten times as much); **fighters keeping room** (`spread`) uses a fixed hash grid instead of making a map each tick; **the census** (`censusStep` in `sim/census.ts`) counts a tenth of the plots each tick and swaps the finished count in every tenth, so no tick pays for all of it (the counts are up to 20 ticks old; `census()` still counts at once for a test or a command); and **the game loop** (`STEP_BUDGET` in `main.ts`) runs ticks for at most 10 ms of a frame (always one if due) and lets go of a backlog it can't catch up on, so an overloaded game runs slower rather than freezing the picture. Result: 51 ms down to 34 ms a tick at 30,000 ticks and 69 down to 60 at 40,000, the slowest tick from 500 ms to about 140, and the early spikes (the census) gone.

## Maps and kingdoms

**New map** on the start card goes round six maps (`MAPS` in `game.ts`): three generated ones, always the same (*Emberland*, *Greywater*, *Highmarch*), then three of the real world: **Balkans & Anatolia**, **Europe** and **The World**.

The real-world maps (`sim/realmap.ts`) are drawn from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public domain): its coastlines, islands and lakes, its deserts, and (but for Europe) its named mountain ranges and plateaus. Each is framed on a bigger grid than a generated map's 400×248, so close capitals have room to grow (`sim/mapview.ts`): the Balkans and Anatolia (560×348 tiles) from the Adriatic to the Caucasus, and the world (640×396) cut through the Bering Strait. The map's size is a live setting (`setGrid` in `core/constants.ts`, `resizeGrid` in `sim/state.ts`): everything sized by the map is made anew when it changes. `tools/realmaps.ts` turns the data into a compact grid of ground kinds in `sim/maps.ts` (`node tools/realmaps.ts <folder of Natural Earth GeoJSON> [map ids]`; it lists the files it needs, and redraws only the maps named). On the Balkans and the world, islands too small to matter are dropped and the rest are joined to the mainland by **land bridges** (sandbars) across the narrowest straits: Dover, the Bosporus, Messina, Korea, Torres and so on (a few drawn by hand: Java along the Lesser Sundas to Australia, and the Americas by Baffin Island, Greenland, Iceland and the Faroes to Scotland), so every kingdom can reach every other.

**Europe is the big one**, made for full 64-kingdom games, and drawn with the most care: **1824×1488 tiles**, a tile about 3 km, twice the scale of the other maps and 30 generated maps' worth, from Iceland to Azerbaijan (the map ends on the Caspian shore, a little past Moscow, with no Kazakhstan and only the west of Russia) and from the Arctic coast to the Nile delta, so 62 countries have their capital on it, from Portugal, Morocco and Egypt to Georgia and Azerbaijan (Iran's Tehran lies just past the edge). With a full game of them each has twice the land of a 10-kingdom generated game, so there is plenty of room to play, and seeing it all means panning about. **The sea is as it is**: no land bridges, so Britain, Ireland, Iceland, the Mediterranean islands and the Aegean's stand on their own (a ship will have to cross; the countries founded there can't reach the mainland yet), drawn at 1:10 million with the minor islands too, Natural Earth's finest lakes (Finland's and the Alps' among them), only specks under 12 tiles and ponds under 6 dropped, and every island with a capital on it kept however small (Malta). **Its relief is real**: mountains, hills and uplands come from elevation (the Terrarium tiles of SRTM, GMTED and ETOPO data, resampled tile by tile by `tools/dem.py`), not from named ranges' outlines: ground above a snow line that falls from 3000 m in the south to 900 m in the far north is high (snow, ringed by rock), mountains are above 70% of it or where the land rises steeply, hills where it rises and falls, and upland plateau where it is high but level, such as the Meseta and Anatolia; then each tile takes the kind most of its neighbours have, so ranges are solid. So the Alps, the Pyrenees, the Carpathian arc, the Dinarides, the Scandinavian fjells and their fjords, the Caucasus are where and what they are, and the plains are plains. Deserts still come from Natural Earth, their outlines frayed into dry land at the edges.

On every real map capitals lie in valleys and on plains: around each one the ranges give way, and a capital by the sea with no room for a castle gets a small harbour filled in. Elsewhere the climate goes smoothly by latitude and distance from the sea: rainforest at the equator, dry subtropics, temperate grass and woods, northern forest, then tundra and ice, in patches of gradient noise turned a little each octave so no grid shows through, as big in tiles on Europe as on the smaller maps (and on Europe, desert only south of 36°N).

**Every kingdom is a real country** (`core/countries.ts`), with its name and its flag, drawn as 12×8 pixel art. The flag gives the kingdom its colour, flies on its leaders' standards and its armies' banners (halved to 6×4), and marks it in the panels and on the leaderboard. **Choosing countries.** The start card shows your country and how many rivals you face; clicking it opens the **Countries** window (`ui/nations.ts`). It lists every country that can play on the map (on a real-world one, those whose capital is on it). Click one to play it, and tick the countries you want to face, or use the quick picks: the nearest 5, 10 or 20 capitals (at random on a generated map), a **full game**, or none. **A game holds at most 64 kingdoms** (`MAX_KINGDOMS` in `core/constants.ts`): you and 63 rivals. *Full game · 64* fills it, choosing the 63 at random when more countries could play (on Europe, 68 can); on a map with fewer it reads *All*, and ticking a rival past the limit is refused. The choice is remembered for each map. On a real-world map, **Start as** founds you at once at your country's real capital, and every rival at its own (close capitals push each other a little apart). On a generated map you still choose where to found your capital, and the rivals are founded around the map. Countries with nearly the same flag colour are told apart by a shade. **The game's speed.** There is one speed (no speed button): the simulation runs two of its seconds in each real second (`GAME_SPEED` in `core/constants.ts`). What the screen shows in time is in real seconds: the clock, *Next human in*, the wage countdown, the income per second and its breakdown, the goods in and out per minute, and the research points a second. The simulation, its tests and the descriptions below count in its own seconds, so a figure like "every 30 s" below is 15 s on the clock.

**Big matches.** A full 64-kingdom game on Europe loads in about a second and runs at 60 frames a second at the game's speed. What makes that possible: each kingdom keeps the box its land lies in, so pushing a border looks only there (`setOwner`, `startAttack` in `sim/territory.ts`); each kingdom knows which goods its land still holds, and a villager with nothing to gather waits a few seconds before looking again (`nearestResources`, `think` in `sim/population.ts`); and the map's pixels go to the screen in 256-pixel squares, only the ones that changed (`flushWorld` in `render/terrain.ts`). The camera never zooms out past the whole map, and keeps it on screen with only a little sea past its edges (`clampCam` in `render/camera.ts`).

**Capitals in their own tradition** (`render/capitals.ts`). Each country builds its capital in its region's style, in all three castle tiers and two states of battle damage. There are 28 of them: the Western keep, Central European baroque, Hanseatic brick, Nordic timber halls, Mediterranean towers, Orthodox domes, Greek whitewash and (at tier 3) a marble temple, the Kremlin, Ottoman domes and minarets, Maghreb kasbahs, Arabian mud forts, a Mesopotamian ziggurat, Egyptian pylons and pyramids, Persian tilework, Mughal marble, Sahel mud mosques, Ethiopian Gondar castles, southern African stone enclosures, Chinese gate towers (in Vietnamese and Korean colours too), Japanese keeps, Siamese and Burmese temples, a Javanese candi, steppe gers, Mesoamerican pyramids, Inca stonework, Spanish colonial cathedrals, the Capitol, and Gothic parliaments. They grow taller and gain towers and domes at tier 2 and gold at tier 3, and every one but the keep flies its country's flag. Damage is worked out from the drawing itself, so it fits any shape: knocked-off tops, cracks, soot, breaches, fires and rubble.

## How it looks

The simulation works in 2 pixels per tile (where people stand). The renderer draws everything at twice that detail (`K`, `RS`, `PS` in `core/constants.ts`): 4×4 pixels per tile, 16×16 per building plot, people 4×8. The camera works in these render pixels. Drawing never changes the simulation, so the look can change freely without touching the determinism test.

- **Tiles of the picture** (`render/tiles.ts`). The map is drawn in square tiles of 256 render pixels (64 map tiles), each with a canvas for the ground, one for nature and one for buildings. A tile is made and painted from the game's state only when it comes on screen (four a frame at most, nearest the middle first, the overview standing in meanwhile), changes are painted into the tiles that exist, and those seen longest ago are let go past a hundred, so the picture costs what the screen shows whatever the map's size: the big Europe holds 60 frames a second in a desktop browser and on a phone, at every zoom. Zoomed far out (a map tile under 2.5 screen pixels), the **overview** is drawn instead: one pixel a map tile, with the ground's colours, the kingdoms and their borders, occupied land, roads, and towns as their roofs.
- **Terrain** (`render/terrain.ts`): a warm, slightly muted palette lit from the top-left; sea with wave dashes, a foam line and wet sand; grass tufts and the odd flower. Kingdoms get a double border: their colour on the edge and a paler line inside. Roads are two-pixel tracks with worn verges.
- **Sprites** (`render/sprites.ts`, `render/nature.ts`, `render/people.ts`) are hand-placed pixel art, many drawn from text grids (`render/pixel.ts`), and outlined automatically where they meet open ground, so towns and crowds stay readable. Buildings and trees are drawn into the tiles of the picture as they are made, and again where they change; people are made once per look and stamped each frame.
- **People** face the way they walk and step as they go. Soldiers wear a tabard and helmet crest in their kingdom's colour and raise a spear, thrust forward on the tick they strike. Each figure is outlined in a dark shade of its kingdom's colour, so mixed crowds stay apart.

## Gold and strength

Each kingdom starts with 250 gold, **10 wood, 10 stone and 10 copper**, and the land within 8 tiles of its castle (`GOODS_START` in `core/constants.ts`, `START_R` in `sim/sim.ts`), and earns from two taxes: **0.1 gold a second from each housed villager** (0.2 and 0.3 with taxation research; soldiers, the homeless and beggars pay none) and a small **land tax of 0.0004 gold a second for every tile it holds** (`LAND_TAX` in `core/constants.ts`: 2 a second for 5,000 tiles, a flat boost for size that makes land worth having beyond the room to build); each coin minter adds 10% to both. **Soldiers are paid wages by what they are doing** (`wageOf` in `sim/war.ts`): **0.22** a second each idle in a barracks (a low-maintenance rate), **0.3** on a border (deployed in peace, massed in a crisis, or given the defend order at war) and **0.4** on the offensive. Militia (below) cost nothing at home, **0.1** called up to a border or defending and **0.2** on the offensive. **Land the enemy occupies pays no land tax.** The treasury can go below zero (the gold turns red); nobody signs up as a soldier while it is, and after 10 seconds unpaid, soldiers start laying down their arms where they stand, one every 3 seconds, the least armoured first (those at home before those away). A deserter's armour is scrapped for 30 gold per tier (60 for reinforced, 90 for heavy). The kingdom card breaks the income down: villager tax, land tax, what the coin minters add, what the soldiers cost and what the militia cost while they are called up (`incomeParts` in `sim/sim.ts`), and counts down when wages go unpaid. Bots keep only as many soldiers as their tax can pay, and at war also what their treasury can pay over a minute and a half (tax alone keeps only about a quarter of a kingdom under arms, since a soldier costs three times what a villager pays). **Settling spreads as a front, never all at once**: the push takes tiles from its edge at random, a little sooner those the border has wound round (up to four tried, the one with most of your land about it taken), so holes fill as it goes, at the usual price and pace. Capturing pockets instantly is for war occupation alone (`encircle` in `sim/war.ts`). Clicking unclaimed land opens a small menu right there: push the border toward it with 25%, 50%, 75% or 100% of the treasury (keys 1 to 4): 0.5 gold a tile. Rough ground settles a little slower, by `TMULT` in `core/constants.ts`, cut by 80% so settling anywhere pays: mountains ×1.4 (were ×3), snowfields ×1.28 (×2.4), hills ×1.16 (×1.8), forest ×1.08, desert ×1.04, beaches ×1.02. Hovering unclaimed land shows it. Unspent gold comes back when a push ends. Gold never buys another kingdom's land: that takes war (below).

**Strength** is what the labels and leaderboard show: 1 per tile, 50 per villager, 100 per soldier (50 per militiaman) and 3 per gold (`STR_*` in `core/constants.ts`). Beggars count for nothing.

## People

The capital releases one human every 20 seconds, 5% sooner per farm (`sim/population.ts`). Each one is a villager or a soldier, and the kingdom keeps the share of soldiers its slider asks for. **No barracks is needed to take up arms**: without one, everyone the slider asks for trains at the castle as **militia** (below), and a barracks then turns them into trained soldiers as it has room. With too few soldiers, newcomers are born soldiers at the barracks, and the villagers nearest a barracks walk in, spend 3 seconds inside, and come out as soldiers; with too many, the nearest soldiers walk in and come out as villagers (those with no room in a barracks, and the least armoured, first).

**Barracks and armies** (`sim/army.ts`). Barracks side by side join into one barracks. Each module houses an army of 5 and has 5 armour racks, so four modules together keep one army of 20. Each barracks' army waits in front of it, and over its roof a label shows the army: soldiers there over its room, then its soldiers by tier, for example `4/5 · 3 I · 1 II`. **The barracks' room is all the trained soldiers a kingdom can have.** Ask for more people under arms than that (the soldier slider counts everyone under arms) and the rest train at the castle as **militia**: with room for 15 and 20 called for, 15 walk to the barracks and 5 to the castle. Militia wear homespun with a sash in the kingdom's colour and carry a pitchfork; they are weaker than soldiers (5 hit points, 0.75 a blow, no armour), pay no tax and draw no wages. At peace they rest in their houses like villagers (those with no house patrol the city), they come out to defend the kingdom when it is at war, and only they guard watchtowers; they don't march with the armies, but the war screen can send them too: under the armies, **Use militia** opens a slider for how many of those at home go (all of them to start with; the nearest the castle first). They go as a band of their own, paid a soldier's wage while out: to the border with an attack. With no barracks, they are all a kingdom can send. They go to war only when **called up as reserves** (below). Soldiers whose barracks burns, or shrinks under them, serve on as militia. A free place in a barracks (a soldier fell, or a new barracks went up) goes to **a militiaman first**: the nearest militia walk over together, one for each free place (a new barracks fills at once), train, and come out soldiers, while the reserves are not called up; with no militia at home, to the next new recruit, never to a watchtower's guard. Only barracks armies go to war, and each barracks sends its own army, mustering in front of it and marching on its own. Two barracks in different places make two separate armies of 5, so it pays to build barracks beside each other for one big army, and keep a smaller one elsewhere. Click a barracks to see its army: how many are there and away, its soldiers by tier, the armour they wear and the armour waiting on the racks, and what the blacksmith is doing for it.

**Soldier tiers.** Every soldier starts at tier 1, archaic: bare-headed, in a tunic of the kingdom's colour, with a wooden spear tipped with flint (8 hit points, 1 damage a blow). Tier 2 is reinforced, in mail and a crested helmet (10 hit points, 1.5 damage). Tier 3 is heavy armour, in plate with a closed, plumed helm and a kite shield in the kingdom's colours (15 hit points, 2 damage). Armour comes from the **blacksmith**: its smith (a villager it takes on, replaced 5 seconds after he falls) waits until infantry research allows it, then fetches 5 copper from the castle, forges for 5 seconds, and carries the piece to the barracks nearest his smithy that still wants armour. A soldier of that barracks who comes by puts on the best piece on its racks that beats what he wears (his hit points rise with it), and his old armour is thrown away. A barracks gets reinforced armour until every rack has some, and only then heavy armour, which replaces it. A soldier who goes back to work leaves his armour on the racks.

Villagers don't gather (nothing lies on the map: see Resources); they build houses, and otherwise rest at home. Soldiers wait in front of their barracks, or guard a watchtower that calls for them, until they are sent to war; militia rest at home too (see below). People only walk inside their own borders (armies in the field aside), go around the castle rather than over it, and die if another kingdom takes the ground they stand on, or they fall in battle (soldiers have 8 to 15 hit points by tier, villagers 4; see below).

## Homes

Every person, villager or soldier, needs a home: a hovel holds one, a stone house two, a manor three (below). Whenever the kingdom has less room in its houses (counting ones being built) than people, a villager's next job is to build one, so a newcomer usually starts by building his own hovel before he goes to work. He picks the plot himself the way towns grow: beside other buildings and roads, outward from the edge of town, near the capital. He walks there carrying a plank, puts up a frame, works 4 seconds, and the hovel stands; new houses connect to the road network. If a house is already free (people died but their homes survived), a newcomer goes straight to work.

With more room than people for 20 seconds, the spare houses stand empty and run down, the farthest from the capital first; as people arrive again, the innermost empty houses are lived in first (`sim/population.ts`). Land caps housing: once a kingdom's plots are full, new people stay homeless until it expands.

**Resting at home.** A villager with a home and nothing to build or do doesn't wander the streets: he walks to the nearest house with a free bed and goes in, out of sight. **Smoke rises only from houses with someone resting inside**, so a smoking chimney means a pair of hands is at home. Resting, he checks every 10 to 12 seconds whether houses need building for the homeless, and the homeless, who have nowhere to rest, look as often (`goRest` in `sim/population.ts`). **The way round the kingdom.** Straight lines (and a detour round the castle) are all a villager's route knew, and in a kingdom with bays, coves and borders the straight way home from a tree is often shut, which left villagers idle with a whole forest in the kingdom. So each kingdom has a **walking field**: the steps from its castle's gate over its own free land, worked out again every 10 seconds for everyone at once (`walkOf`, `fillWalk` in `sim/population.ts`). What the field doesn't reach isn't looked for, and what it reaches is walked to down the field and out again, pulled straight where the line is clear (`routeField`). Builders, smiths, resting villagers and soldiers walking home use the field when the straight way is shut; a house whose door the field doesn't reach is no home to go to. Enemies at war who find a house with people resting inside go for it (attackers along the front): when they break it open, the people come out of the rubble beside it and fight (`evict` in `sim/war.ts`). On a 60-kingdom Europe at minute 15, about half the people rest at home (the rest are gathering), and a tick of the simulation takes 10 to 12 ms instead of 18.

The people who have lived in the kingdom longest get the houses, so the newest arrivals are the homeless ones, and whoever has been homeless longest moves in first when a house comes free. Homeless villagers pay no tax. A villager homeless for a minute turns **beggar**: he crawls slowly about town in rags, does no work, pays no tax and can't be recruited, until a home comes free for him and he goes back to work. **Half a minute later (a minute and a half without a roof) he dies of want**, dropping where he stands like the fallen of a battle, but without a wound or a pool of blood (`STARVE_TICKS` in `sim/population.ts`); the log tells you when your own begin to die. Homeless soldiers stay soldiers.

## Buildings

Houses are built by villagers (above). Everything else is placed by the kingdom from the four building menus on the right-hand buttons (B reopens the last one) and paid for from its stockpile: **urban development** (farm, coin minter, builders guild), **military** (barracks, watchtower, blacksmith, naval base), **resource buildings** (plantation, stone quarry, copper mine) and **society advancement** (the research station, for now). There are no zoom buttons (the mouse wheel and pinch zoom) and no centre button (Space centres on your capital):

| Building | Cost (wood / stone / copper) | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Farm | 5 / 2 / 0 | New humans arrive 5% sooner per farm; **each farm you own makes the next 5% dearer** (so 10 farms: the next costs 50% more; `RISE` in `sim/buildings.ts`) |
| Barracks | 5 / 5 / 0 | Trains soldiers and houses an army of 5 with 5 armour racks. Barracks side by side join into one bigger army. Click it to see its army |
| Watchtower | 2 / 5 / 1 | At war, shoots the nearest enemy soldier in range on your land, or else the nearest enemy villager there (2, 3 or 4 damage every 4, 3.5 or 3 s by its tier; 20, 30 or 40 blows to burn; see the defences below). Click it to toggle **!**: it then calls 5 militia (soldiers without room in a barracks) to guard it. Several calling towers share the idle militia out one at a time in the order they called, so 5 soldiers for 2 towers go 3 and 2 |
| Coin minter | 5 / 5 / 5 | +10% gold income; **each one you own makes the next 10% dearer** |
| Plantation | 10 / 0 / 0 | One plot. Saplings grow into trees over 30 s: +2 wood, more when joined (below); each one makes the next 10% dearer |
| Stone quarry | 5 / 10 / 0 | One plot. A pulley hauls stone out of the pit: +2 stone every 30 s, more when joined; each one makes the next 10% dearer |
| Copper mine | 10 / 10 / 0 | One plot. A shaft glowing orange: +1 copper every 30 s, more when joined; each one makes the next 10% dearer |
| Blacksmith | 10 / 15 / 5 | One villager becomes its smith, who forges armour for the barracks once infantry research allows it, 5 copper a piece |
| Naval base | 15 / 5 / 0 | 8 plots (4×2 or 2×4), a third of them at least on the water. A harbour: build ships here (5 wood, 15 s each; a ship carries 5 soldiers), send a ship to colonize an island, and sail naval invasions (below) |
| Research station | 15 / 15 / 10 | +1 research point a second (below). Click it to open the research panel |
| Builders guild | 10 / 20 / 5 | 2×1 plots. One villager becomes its builder, who rebuilds watchtowers and houses (below); the first one unlocks the city investment slider; each one makes the next 10% dearer |

**Building over buildings.** You can place a building over your own buildings: whatever stands under it (houses, workshops, towers, a frame going up) is pulled down when it goes up (the placing banner says so), and you pay only for the new one. Only the castle and buildings on fire can't be built over. Bots do the same with houses that stand where they want a wall tower or a workshop (`Over`, `demolish` in `sim/buildings.ts`); the people who lived there build anew when they run short of room. **Every plot of land can be built on**, mountains and snowfields too: the high ground isn't wasted space any more. Buildings run by themselves; only the builders guild and the blacksmith need a worker. They are modular: a plot draws itself from its four neighbours, so fields of farms merge into one farmland with a single fence round the outside, barracks and minters next to each other become one long hall, and neighbouring watchtowers are joined by walls (`render/sprites.ts`). **The walls are real**: while both towers stand, nobody but their kingdom and its allies can cross one (`buildWalls` in `sim/defense.ts`). Armies go round a wall if they can; walled out completely, they knock down a tower holding it up, and the wall falls with it. A ring of towers makes a city wall. **Resource gathering.** Plantations, quarries and copper mines are placed one plot each, and two side by side stay two buildings until *resource gathering* research reaches tier 2: then every pair of single ones of a kind side by side joins into one 2×1 building, at once and whenever another is placed. At tier 3, any four of a kind filling a 2×2 square (singles, a pair and two singles, two pairs) join into one 2×2 building. A joined building makes far more than its parts did apart:

| Every 30 s | 1 plot (tier 1) | 2×1 (tier 2) | 2×2 (tier 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plantation | 2 wood | 5 wood | 15 wood |
| Stone quarry | 2 stone | 5 stone | 15 stone |
| Copper mine | 1 copper | 3 copper | 10 copper |

A joined building is one building: the top-left plot holds it and the rest point to it, and if any of its plots is taken, the whole of it burns. One standing on ground the enemy holds makes nothing.

**How much buildings take.** Before they burn: a hovel 10 blows (a stone house 30, a manor 60), a plantation, quarry or copper mine 15 per tier, a farm, coin minter, research station, builders guild or blacksmith 15, a barracks 20, watchtowers and the castle by tier (below). Bots build with simple priorities of their own (`sim/bots.ts`).

## Research, better homes and armour

Each research station earns 1 research point a second, and all of them go to the one topic the kingdom is studying: **housing**, **infantry**, **watchtowers**, the **castle** or **taxation** (`sim/research.ts`). Clicking a station opens the research panel: a button per topic with its tier, and a progress bar below; the lit one is being studied, and clicking the other switches (progress on both is kept). Housing is studied first, from the moment the first station stands. Every topic starts at tier 1 and goes up to tier 3: 200 points for tier 2 and 400 more for tier 3 (infantry: 400, then 600; watchtowers, the castle and resource gathering: 400, then 800; `TIER_XP` in `sim/research.ts`). A finished topic gives way to the next. What is learned is kept for good, even if every station burns.

- **Infantry** lets the blacksmith forge reinforced armour (tier 2) and then heavy armour (tier 3) for the barracks (above).
- **Watchtowers** lets the builders rebuild each watchtower a tier up, and the **castle** rebuilds the castle at once, free (`sim/defense.ts`):

  | | Shoots every | Damage | Blows to break |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Watchtower, tier 1 / 2 / 3 | 4 / 3.5 / 3 s | 2 / 3 / 4 | 20 / 30 / 40 |
  | Castle, tier 1 / 2 / 3 | 2 / 1.75 / 1.5 s (like two towers) | 2 / 3 / 4 | 200 / 400 / 600 |

  The castle mends itself, 2 blows a second; towers don't. A tower is rebuilt taller with a timber gallery and a pointed roof at tier 2, and as a pale keep with a spire, banners and braziers at tier 3; the castle becomes a fortress, its towers roofed and a turret on the keep, then a citadel trimmed in gold. A tower looks battered from half its blows; the castle looks damaged from a third and badly damaged from two thirds, with breaches, soot and fires.
- **Taxation** raises each villager's tax by 0.1 gold a second per tier: 0.1, 0.2, 0.3.
- **Housing** lets the builders guild rebuild houses, and **watchtowers** lets it rebuild watchtowers a tier up (10 stone and 4 wood to tier 2, 20 stone and 8 wood to tier 3), towers first. The hovels villagers put up are tier 1 and they keep building hovels; at tier 2 the guild can turn them into **stone houses** (room for 2, three times the blows to burn), and at tier 3 into **manors** (room for 3, six times a hovel's).
- **Resource gathering** lets plantations, quarries and copper mines join (above).

A **builders guild** takes the villager nearest it as its builder; he wears a leather cap. When research allows, he walks from building to building and rebuilds each one a tier, working 5 seconds with his hammer: the watchtowers first (the nearest the castle first), then the houses, for 3 wood and 3 stone a tier.

**City investment.** The first builders guild unlocks the city investment slider (`sim/ledger.ts`). Beside it, the goods of the last minute: coming in (villagers bringing them home, plantations, quarries and mines; green) and spent by the builders and smiths (red). At **100% the city is in full investment** (the slider reads *Full*): the builders ignore what comes in and improve buildings non-stop with anything in the stockpile, and every house is to become a manor in the end, not just the innermost 40%. Below that, the slider is the share of what comes in that the builders may spend: a builder only starts on another building while what has been spent this past minute, plus its cost, stays within that share. At 50% with 30 wood a minute coming in, the builders spend up to 15 wood a minute and the other half goes into the stockpile. Bots invest 58% to 78% by temper. He rebuilds the town from the castle outward, so it looks grown rather than planned: with manors allowed, the innermost 40% of houses (the edge left a little ragged) are to become manors and the rest stone houses, and the nearest house short of what it should be goes first. An improved house is as good as new. The builder runs or fights when enemies come like any villager; if he falls, another villager takes up his tools 5 seconds later. Several guilds work side by side. Bots build stations once they have found their feet, study whichever topic is behind, and build guilds once they can use them.

## Diplomacy and war

Clicking another kingdom's land opens diplomacy (`ui/diplomacy.ts`, `sim/war.ts`):

- **Alliance.** A bot agrees unless it has 3 allies already, you attacked it in the last 90 seconds, or you are less than 0.4 of its strength. Allies are named in green, on the map and the leaderboard, and open their land to each other's armies. Declaring war on an ally ends the alliance.
- **War.** A second screen lists your armies, one per barracks, each with its soldiers at home and by tier; pick which go (all of them to start with). Each one marches on its own. Then choose:
  - **Naval invasion**: needs a **naval base** with enough **ships** (5 soldiers each, from the kingdom's fleet: any base may send them) and a coast of theirs on the same sea. Choosing it doesn't start a crisis: the war screen shows your naval bases and the ships ready, and the ships the armies chosen need (`⚓ Naval invasion`). Pick **one base**, then **Choose the coastline…** and point at their coast: hovering shows the **landing zone** before you click, a man every 2 tiles of coast (a bigger army takes more of it) with the ground inland of it, and the ships in line off it. Clicking sends the ships and the armies aboard: it is a **crisis** as for an attack, the ships lying in a line off the coast; the defender sees the zone marked and may send its armies to stand on it. At the word, the zone is occupied and the men step ashore, row after row (a short coast takes as many ships to a row as it has room for, the next row landing after), and the ships sail home and are counted again when they dock. After that it is an ordinary war from the beachhead. **An army landed by sea is never reinforced**: more must be sent from the war screen, by land or by sea. A naval invasion onto a coast you already hold needs no crisis and no word, and the defender cannot respond to it. See *The sea* below.
  - **Attack**: needs a shared border (a landing by sea makes its own). It starts as a **crisis** (below): your armies mass on their border, but it is not yet war; your word turns it into a **war** that lasts until peace, whatever happens to the armies. It is fought on a **front** along the whole border (below). The army **occupies** the ground it takes (hatched with "/" lines in the occupier's colour); what stands there isn't burnt, but nobody lives, gathers or builds there, and occupied land still belongs to its kingdom until peace. Breaking the castle (200 to 600 blows by its tier, and it mends) **annexes** the kingdom: its land, buildings and people become yours, and its gold and stockpile are lost.

Two kingdoms are **at war** while a war between them lasts; only then do soldiers fight and watchtowers shoot. A crisis is not a war. (There are no raids: an army either fights on a front or lands from the sea.) Each war has a button at the top of the screen:

- your crisis: **De-escalate**. Calls it off: your armies go home (except those you had deployed on the border in peace), and so do those they sent to their border, with nothing to make peace over. Once you begin the offensive it is war, and only peace ends it;
- their crisis on your border: **Respond** opens a menu: **Hold the border…** picks which of your armies (all of them, to start with) go to stand on your side of the border, facing theirs. In a crisis they don't fight: only the side that massed first can begin the offensive, 30 seconds in at the soonest;
- a war: **offer peace**. Bots weigh an offer by how the war is going (armies, land, how long it has lasted): they refuse while they think they can win and accept when they are in trouble. At peace each side keeps the land it occupies, but only what **joins on to its own land**: a pocket of occupied land closed in by the other kingdom, with no way through occupied ground to the occupier's own, goes back to its owner, so peace never leaves islands inside a border (`cede` in `sim/war.ts`). **A capital cut off** (all the ground round the castle occupied) is given room to rebuild: at peace, whatever of its land the other side holds within 20 tiles of the castle goes back to it first (`giveRoom`), and only then is the rest settled as above, so a beaten kingdom is never left with only its castle. Bots also offer peace when losing, and when a war has **stalled**: its border hardly moving for three minutes (`watchBorders` in `sim/war.ts` looks every 30 seconds) while one side has lost more than twice the other's dead, or for six minutes whatever the dead. Then they stop sending armies to it, hold their front and send their reserves home, and a stalled war makes either side readier to accept, so no war grinds on for ever with endless reinforcements. The button then accepts it;
- an enemy army massing on your border or landing from the sea, or at war: **Respond** opens a menu under the button: request peace (in a war only) or **Send armies to the front…** (as *Hold the border* in a crisis; against a landing, the armies stand on the coast it will land on);
- your land all taken back after an invasion: **Counter-attack or peace** opens a menu: offer them peace, or **counter-attack**, which opens the war screen with every army. Now you are the attacker, and they can intercept you. A counter-attack is driven by revenge: the log says *your troops are inspired and thirsty for revenge*, and its soldiers hit 25% harder and march 10% faster. On the march they shout for it in little speech bubbles over their heads (*Once more into the breach!*, *They will pay for that mistake!*, …; `render/speech.ts`, which holds the lines for each situation).

**The front.** A war is fought on a **front**, as in Hearts of Iron (`sim/front.ts`, `runOffensives` in `sim/war.ts`). The front is where the two kingdoms meet: every tile of the enemy's land touching ground the attacker holds, its own or occupied. The armies sent march to it and **spread out along the whole of it**, evenly, just on their own side (a tile back from the line, so the land they hold ends barely ahead of them: tested at under 1.2 tiles from a man in his place): a front rank of one man every 2 tiles or more, covering the border end to end, each army keeping its own stretch in the order its barracks lie. Men the line has no room for form **ranks behind it**: each man of the front rank has his reserves behind him, in file, or in a little block when there are more than five, so the man behind steps straight into the place of the one who falls. The line is worked out again twice a second as it moves, and each man keeps his place in order along it, so nobody crosses over his neighbours.

**Crisis, then war.** Declaring an attack doesn't start a war: it starts a **crisis**. Your armies march to the border and spread out along it, and the defender may send armies to its own side of the same border, the two lines facing each other a few tiles apart; nobody fights, the watchtowers hold their fire, and the war chip reads *Crisis*. **Armies deployed on the border beforehand** (below) are already there: a crisis begun with them needs no march, and only the further armies you choose arrive. A crisis runs at least **30 seconds** (`CRISIS_TICKS`): the button under the chip reads *⏳ Time to talk · 24 s · 35/38 in place* and cannot be pressed, time for both nations to react, answer, or de-escalate (calling it off leaves the armies deployed in peace where they stand). When it is up the button becomes a pulsing **⚔ Begin offensive**: click it and it is war, with every army that attacks going in at once while the others defend; it doesn't turn the crisis into a war by itself, so the choice stays yours. Bots wait the same 30 seconds before they begin, or call it off if far outmatched. A war already under way has no crisis: armies sent to it fight on your orders.

**Orders per army.** Every barracks' army, and the militia as one army, has its own orders (the **Armies** list below). At war each army **attacks** or **defends**. An attacking army pushes the front on all along its stretch, and, while enemy soldiers hold some of its kingdom's land on that front, takes that land back first. A **defending army falls back to the closest ground of its own that no enemy tower reaches** (the edge of the ground it holds, short of tower range, occupied ground included: `safeLine` in `sim/war.ts`) and forms a line there, fighting whatever comes to it and never going out after the enemy; give it **⚔ Attack** again and it forms up on the front line and goes in, as an organised attack. The aggressor's armies attack by default, and a defender's defend (taking back its land, if the enemy has taken any). The war bar's **Attack all** and **Defend all** give the word to every army at once. **An invading army moves half as fast** on enemy ground it has not occupied yet (a strip is taken every 2 seconds, not every second, and the men march at half pace: `ADVANCE_TICKS`, `INVADE_SLOW`, `marchPace` in `sim/war.ts`), so the defender has time to react; units going to ground already occupied are not slowed.

**Retaking the borders.** While an enemy occupies land of a defender's on a front, the defender's armies on that front that **attack** take it back first, pressing along the occupied zone (and only that: never into the enemy's own land) until every tile of it is back (`liberating` in `sim/war.ts`); an unordered defender does this by itself, and one told to defend holds its line instead. While they retake it, and after, its men fight only enemies standing on their own kingdom's land (occupied or not), never chase one out onto the enemy's ground, go for no watchtower or wall there, and take none of the enemy's land. Once it is all back they go on defending on friendly ground out of reach of the enemy's towers until you give **⚔ Attack**. There is **one general front line per stretch of border**, drawn on the map as a line of tiles in the attacker's colour with a dark edge (it pulses while the front attacks, and is steady while it holds: `frontLines`, `drawFronts` in `render/render.ts`). **A pocket of occupied land cut off** from the occupier's main body, by the defenders retaking the ground between, **goes back to the defender at once** (`returnIslands`).

**Several fronts.** Two kingdoms may meet on more than one landmass (a colony across the sea against the other's mainland, say). Each stretch of border is its **own front**, by landmass (`frontInfo`, `lineOf` in `sim/front.ts`; the occupied land is counted by landmass in `W.occIsle`): every army is on one front, and chooses which in the Armies list (*North front*, *West front*, …), each with its own attack or defend. An army attacking on one front doesn't touch the others. An enemy's land across the sea is taken only by the army sent to it (the *cut-off pockets* rule works only on the landmass of the castle), and when nothing of theirs is left to take on a front, **its army has done its job and is unassigned**: back at its barracks, free to be sent to the other front or sent anywhere. Landing from the sea on a coast of your own that they occupy joins that front, and its army takes the land back; landing on any other coast of theirs makes a front of its own.

**How the front moves.** Every 2 seconds (every second for a capital rush) the front takes a strip of the ground before it, all along the line, so the occupied land grows as an even band and the line stays straight, like the even spread of OpenFront or Territorial.io, but with every man on it there and fighting. Each man of the front rank carries about 4 tiles of line at full speed: a line spread thinner moves slower in proportion, and a stretch with nobody on it doesn't move. A stretch that has fallen behind (held up by defenders, or with further to go) catches up, up to 3 tiles a strip, and any corner where the line has dropped back fills in, so the front evens itself out instead of growing teeth and dents. **A soldier holds the ground about him** (2 tiles): ground a defender stands on can't be taken, so the line stops there and fights until he is beaten or driven off, then the gap closes up with the rest. Walls and the castle can't be walked through. Enemy ground cut off behind the front, closed in by the attacking side's land or open land, falls to it at once, so no pockets are left behind. **A surrounded capital.** When the ground round the castle is all occupied (nothing of the defender's free in the ring of tiles beside it: `castleCutOff` in `sim/front.ts`), the armies **stop pushing the border** and the front becomes the castle alone: every man goes to break it, and nothing else of the defender's land is taken meanwhile. **Two kingdoms attacking one.** Armies of kingdoms at war with the same target (and not with each other) share it: the ground one of them occupies is its own, and the free ground beyond it is split in slices round the target's castle, cut by the bearings of the attackers' castles, so each takes its slice (both fronts run along the same edge of the occupied ground, each in its own part of it). An attacker whose own border is shut in by the other's occupation walks through it to its slice, and one with nothing in its slice goes for any free ground rather than going home (`lineOf` in `sim/front.ts`). The tests check all of this on a straight border and on borders at 25° and 45° (`describe('the front')` in `tests/war.test.ts`): measured row by row, the line never strays more than 3 or 4 tiles from a straight one, no ground is left behind it, a thin line is slower, a held front takes nothing, reserves step up, cut-off ground falls, and a 100 against 100 battle on a front runs at under a millisecond a tick. `describe('crisis')` checks that massed armies don't fight until the word, that only the side that massed first can give it or call it off, that a bot backs down when outmatched, and that a defender's counter-attack takes its own land back before anything of the attacker's and moves on as straight a line.

**Stances.** Three buttons under the war chip say how the armies on the front fight (`stance` in `sim/war.ts`, the `S_*` constants): **Balanced** (the default) takes the border all along the front and brings down watchtowers as it meets them; **Border capture** takes as much ground as it can but keeps out of reach of crowded watchtowers (three or more within shooting reach of each other) and leaves those towers alone, to lose fewer men; **Capital rush** pushes a narrow way (14 tiles either side) straight for their capital, twice as often as a balanced strip, takes the ground about the capital, and brings down only the watchtowers by the way or by the capital, to end the war quickly. A bot picks one every ten seconds: a rush when its men are over twice what the other side defends with and it is bold, a border capture against eight or more watchtowers, else balanced.

**Reinforcements, never retreat.** Every soldier a barracks raises goes straight to its army on the front, to wherever the line is thinnest. **New soldiers join the army that has lost the most compared to its size** (its free places over its room), one at a time, so the armies are made up evenly and the one bled hardest first (`station` in `sim/army.ts`). Nobody retreats to make good his losses: whether to defend or keep pushing is up to you. **Across the sea.** An army whose front is on another landmass than its barracks (a colony, say) is carried over: its men, and every reinforcement after them, walk to a **naval base** of yours on their own landmass and wait at its door; a **transport** (a ship of the fleet) sails **only when it holds 5** (`SHIP_CAP`), to a coast of yours on the front's landmass, puts them ashore to march on to the front, and sails home to be counted again (`ferryShips` in `sim/war.ts`). With no naval base, or no sea route, an army can't be sent there. An army that has lost every man stays in the field while the war lasts and its barracks stands, so new soldiers keep joining it: with the reserves at home, the militia fill the fallen's places in the barracks one at a time and march to the front as soldiers. **Call reserves**, a button under the balance of power (in a crisis or at war), sends every militiaman at home but the watchtowers' guards to the front at once, and those raised while they are out follow; where some already went with the attack, it reads **Call the rest** and sends the others to join them; called up they are paid a soldier's wage, they die easily, and their work is lost, so call them when the line really needs them. The war bar lists them as *Reserves*; **Send reserves home** stands them down. Bots call theirs up only when their armies on a front are badly outnumbered (1.3 times at war with their land being taken, or twice; in a crisis, 1.5 times all they have) and send them home once the front is safe.

**Leaders.** An army of more than 5 sets out under a leader, ranked by its size when it leaves (`RANKS`, `rankFor` in `sim/war.ts`): a **Leader** for 6 to 15 soldiers, a **General** for 16 to 30, a **Captain** for 31 to 50, a **Commander** for 51 to 80 and a **Grand Marshal** for 81 and more. Five or fewer have none. The best armoured soldier leads (the longest serving among equals). He is the best protected man in the army: his hit points double for each rank (a Leader 2×, a General 4×, a Captain 8×, a Commander 16×, a Grand Marshal 32× a soldier's of his armour), though he hits no harder, and he goes back to a soldier's once home. He carries a standard in his kingdom's colours with his rank in gold pips, and close up his rank is written under his feet. On the front he stands behind the middle of his men, a rank behind the last, fights only whoever is on him, and his men turn first on anyone attacking him. He calls out what the army is doing in speech bubbles (marching, in position, attacking, holding), and the log tells you what matters to you, for your own armies and for armies attacking you. If he falls, his men fight on without him.

**The Armies list.** Armies are **Army 1, Army 2, …** (numbered when first seen) until you name one: the **✎** button in the list's header asks you to click an army, then for its new name (an empty name gives the number back); names change only by your hand. A list at the top right (under the kingdoms) shows every army of yours in peace and in war: each barracks' army, and the militia as one. Click one for its orders. **At home**: for each neighbouring kingdom, **Deploy on the border** (with one button for each stretch of border when you meet them on several landmasses): the army gathers on the border as a precaution. Deploying is **not a crisis**: nobody fights, the other side sees armies at its border but that is all, and you can **Recall** them. A crisis begun later uses them where they stand, and only the armies you add arrive. Once a crisis or war has begun with that kingdom the button reads **Send to the front**. An army already out on a border (deployed in peace, or standing against someone's crisis) can be sent on to another kingdom's border from where it stands, and the war screen and the *Respond* menu list it with the rest; so two kingdoms massing on you at once are answered separately, each with the armies you pick. An army deployed in peace keeps strictly to your own land, follows only the free land of theirs that touches yours (not what a third kingdom occupies of it), and when no such border is left it has done its order and goes back to its barracks. **Out**: its front (a button per stretch of border), and in a war **⚔ Attack** and **🛡 Defend**; in peace, **Recall**. Right-click one of your leaders to open his army's row: his soldiers are ringed in gold. Esc or right-clicking elsewhere lets go. The barracks panel shows what its army is doing out there and under whom (`ui/command.ts`). The war chip, its word and its menu **go the moment the war is over**: armies deployed in peace, or on their way home, leave nothing on the screen.

**Defending.** A kingdom at war that has sent no army of its own to the border isn't left bare: the trained soldiers it has at home (not militia, who wait to be called up) man **their side of the front**, spread along it the same way with their reserves behind them, and walk to it the shortest way through their own land. There they fight whoever comes within reach of their places, hold the ground about them, and take back any of their land the attacker doesn't hold. Once the invaders are gone the guard takes the occupied land back (the part nearest the capital first). **Occupied land stays occupied** until a soldier of its owner comes and takes it back, or peace: ground out of the guard's reach no longer falls back by itself, so the front doesn't lose what it has taken behind it. When every tile is back, the kingdom is told its army is ready to counter-attack (bots then counter-attack if they can, or ask for peace). Watchtower guards stay by their towers, fighting enemies near them, first those attacking buildings or people.

**Villagers** near an enemy decide, once, at even odds: run (they flee from the danger, staying on their land) or take up arms (a pitchfork). The armed ones fall in behind the soldiers of the home guard line, and fight only whoever comes at them; with no line, they go for the nearest enemy. **Beggars strike no blow**: they always go, but only to stand in the enemy's way, 4 hit points of human shield that the enemy has to cut down. A while after the last enemy has gone, everyone goes back to work.

**Fighting.** Soldiers have 8, 10 or 15 hit points by tier and villagers 4. A soldier strikes once a second, hitting 70% of the time for 1, 1.5 or 2 by tier; a militiaman hits for 0.75 and a villager for 0.5. **Fights are duels**: when two men fight, each is the other's only opponent, and nobody else may strike either of them (`partner`, `locked` in `sim/war.ts`). A man who finds his enemy already fighting somebody picks another, or waits in his place in the line, and the next man takes a fallen man's place only once he is dead; a man who runs more than a few pixels from his duel is free of it. So an overwhelming army can't swarm the few who stand against it: it fights them one on one, and the war is slower. Men in a duel don't push the front forward before them (the line closes over the gap when it ends), and a villager still runs or fights at the sight of any enemy. Watchtowers still shoot whoever they please. Watchtowers shoot every 8, 7 or 6 s for 2, 3 or 4 damage by tier (range 16 tiles; half as often as they once did, `TOWER_RELOADS` in `sim/defense.ts`), the castle twice as often for the same (range 20 tiles); invaders go for any watchtower that can reach them before anything else, to stop the arrows; both shoot only at enemies standing on their own kingdom's land, soldiers before villagers, and their bolts arc visibly to the target. A fighter lunges at his enemy as he strikes; whoever is hit flashes white and staggers back. Everyone heals 0.1 a second. Wounds show: soldiers look hurt (blood, a dented helmet, torn mail) below two thirds of their hit points and near death below a third, head sunk, leaving a short trail of blood; villagers look bloodied below 60%. The fallen drop where they stood, in a spreading pool of blood, and fade after half a minute.

**The balance of power.** Under each war button, a bar shows the current battle: each side in its colour, as wide as its share of everyone who has fought in it (the armies sent, and anyone at home pulled into the fight), with that side's dead blacked out under skulls from its end of the bar. Below it, each side's numbers: how many are still fighting, soldiers and villagers dead, and each of its armies in the fight, such as *West army 4/10 +1*: 4 men with the army, room for 10 in its barracks, and 1 reinforcement on the way. A new battle starts when an army sets out after a lull.

**Story events** (`sim/story.ts`, told in the message log by `ui/story.ts`) are moments in a kingdom's life that change what its people do. Each has a check, run every so often for every kingdom, and a cooldown. The first: **the mob**. When enemy soldiers reach the castle, or burn a crowded part of town, the villagers nearby drop everything and swarm them, whatever they would have chosen alone.

**The sea** (`sim/naval.ts`). A **naval base** (military menu, 15 wood and 5 stone) is a harbour of **8 plots**, 4×2 or turned 2×4 to fit, **a third of them at least on the water**; any land in it must be your own and some of it must be land. It is drawn with a plank quay along the shore, piers running out, a warehouse, a crane. Click it for the **ship yard**: *Build a ship · 5 wood* takes 15 seconds on the clock (`SHIP_TICKS`), a slider shows the ship in the yard, and the panel counts ships ready, being built and at sea. The fleet belongs to the kingdom, not to a base: every base may send any of it. Ships sail only on water, routed over the sea from the base's dock (`seaNearest`, `seaPaths`), and sail home to dock after their work; a ship at sea doesn't count as ready. **Naval settlement**: the settle menu (click unclaimed land) ends in a single **⚓** button; with no naval base or no ship ready it is grayed out and says what is missing (*Build a naval base to settle by sea*, *You need a ship*), and land of an island where you have none can only be settled this way. Click it, then click the land to send the ship to (a green mark shows where it would land, red where it cannot): only then does the menu come back with the 25% to 100% buttons for how much of your gold the ship carries. On an island, a ship of the fleet sails from the nearest base to the coast nearest the click, at any distance; on land joined to your own, the click must be within 10 tiles of the shore (`COAST_REACH`), else *Too far from the coast*. The ship puts **one tile** ashore, the gold spreads the border **from that tile** like any settling (over its own landmass: `Attack.seed`), and the ship sails home and counts again when it docks. From then on the land is settled like the rest, and a plain settle spreads over mainland and island alike. A **sea lane** opens from the base to the landing, and villagers cross it **in small boats**, 4 times as fast as on foot, to found the new village and build houses there; they need no ship. Ships and invasions: see *War* above.

**Bots** plan their wars ahead. They won't start one before their treasury holds a **war chest**: two minutes of what an army for it would cost beyond their tax, with 60% of their people under arms and the tax of the villagers who take them up lost (`warChest` in `sim/bots.ts`; half that when beggars drive them to it). From a minute before they mean to go to war, with an army ready and everyone housed, they settle only with the gold beyond the chest. At war they pay wages from the chest as well as their tax, so they don't go bankrupt. They go to war with their biggest army first and, once it is war, send the others after it in waves, one every 20 to 50 seconds while the attack goes their way (never an army of fewer than 3). Facing a crisis or a front on their border, they send armies to hold it, a fifth more men than are massed against them where they can; and they arm up in a crisis as at war. **Beggars in the streets** (a tenth of their people or more without a home) change their plans: they study housing first, put a builders guild up as soon as housing research allows it (a research station first, to get there), let the builders spend everything coming in, build no more farms (which bring people faster), keep their last free plots for houses, and settle new land with whatever gold they have. With no open land left and their houses as good as they get (or after two minutes of trying), there is no peaceful way out: they rest less between wars and take worse odds, since a war leaves homes behind. With more homes than people, on the other hand, they build what they need over a house, an empty one first. And with land lying empty and everyone housed, they grow their fields together into big joined farmland rather than scattered single farms. They **fortify by degrees** (`sim/fort.ts`): once the town is worth guarding (sooner with neighbours), they plan a town wall, a square ring of watchtowers round the castle far enough out to enclose most of the town, on a ring that runs clear of buildings and other kingdoms' land, with a gate left open in the middle of each side. They raise it a tower at a time: every 45 seconds or so at peace with nobody next door, 24 with neighbours, 10 at war, 6 at war and outnumbered (twice as fast with stone to spare), the side facing their neighbours first, each tower beside the last where it can. They build quarries to feed it, and when threatened, hold stone back from lesser works for it. Their houses and other buildings keep off the line of the wall; workshops gather inside, fields outside. When the town outgrows the wall they start a new ring further out, the old one standing as an inner wall. Towers at the end of a stretch of wall, flanking a gate or a gap, call for a garrison (the "!"), manned by soldiers without room in a barracks; a lone tower out on the border is an outpost and doesn't. Strong walls also deter: bots weigh a kingdom's towers before attacking it. They add barracks modules beside their biggest barracks as their army grows, so it marches as one big army, build a blacksmith once infantry research lets them, and study whichever topic is furthest behind. They keep few soldiers in peace (12% to 27% by temper) so they grow, arm to at least 35% when a much bigger army sits next door, and at war raise 45% to 85% depending on how outnumbered they are, changing quickly up and slowly down. Badly outnumbered, they stay home: no new campaigns, and no counter-attacks. Otherwise they go to war when they have free soldiers to spare, choosing the weakest kingdom in reach (one already beset by two armies is left alone, an old enemy is preferred), attacking a neighbour. They meet an attack on the front, rest between campaigns, and sue for peace when a war goes badly. Bots build no naval bases and invade by land only, but defend against a landing from the sea like against any crisis.

## Resources

**There are no resources on the map.** No trees, rocks or ore lie about to be gathered, and no villager goes out to fetch anything: the whole stockpile of wood, stone and copper comes from the three gathering buildings a kingdom builds (below), and every villager stays in his house until something is wanted of him. (`sim/resources.ts` is just the three goods now; the map shows only the cacti of the deserts.) This keeps a big game light: nobody searches the land for work, nobody carries a load, and about two thirds of the people are out of sight and out of the simulation's way at any time.

**The first plantation, quarry and copper mine a kingdom builds are free** (`costFor` in `sim/buildings.ts`), so every kingdom can start its stockpile; the next ones cost what their cards say, **and each one a kingdom owns makes the next of its kind 10% of its base price dearer** (5% for a farm; a joined building counts for the plots it covers; `RISE`, `costFor` in `sim/buildings.ts`), so a kingdom spreads over kinds rather than piling up one. Each makes goods every 30 seconds by its tier, and with resource gathering research two side by side join (tier 2), then four in a square (tier 3) into one that makes much more than the parts:

| Building | Cost | Alone | Two joined | Four in a square |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plantation | 10 wood | +2 wood | +5 | +15 |
| Stone quarry | 5 wood, 10 stone | +2 stone | +5 | +15 |
| Copper mine | 10 wood, 10 stone | +1 copper | +3 | +10 |

**Bots plan for this** (`groundwork` in `sim/bots.ts`): before anything else they build plantations (a plantation pays for itself in two and a half minutes), then quarries and copper mines, and a research station; they save up for these (nothing else is bought with the goods they need), place each kind side by side and then in squares (`squareFill`), and study resource gathering as soon as they have two of them, to join them. They also build more farms than before, each 5% dearer than the last: farms are what make a kingdom grow, with more people and more taxes.

## The determinism test

`tests/determinism.test.ts` plays three scripted 5-minute matches on the simulation alone and compares fingerprints of the full game state (ownership, troops, buildings, roads, fires, attacks) every 20 seconds of game time against `tests/golden.json`. A pass means the game still plays exactly as it did when the file was recorded. It was first recorded from the original single-file version, and re-recorded when the map grew to 400×248 tiles, when gold replaced troops, when the ocean shrank, when war replaced buying enemy land (it now fingerprints the armies in the field too), when wars became lasting with occupation, when wild resources were halved, and when wars moved to fronts (and mountains became buildable, settling got cheaper on rough ground, and buildings could go over buildings), when wars began with a crisis and the militia came in, when beggars began to die of want, bots learned to end stalled wars, and fronts held their ground a tile closer to their men, and most recently when villagers began to rest at home, felled trees vanished and bots began saving a war chest. (The matches are on generated maps: Europe's redrawing didn't change them, nor did the armies' paths being worked out over the box round the kingdoms concerned, `sim/field.ts`, rather than the whole map.)

If you change gameplay on purpose, the test will fail. That's expected. Re-record the golden file with:

```sh
UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 npm test
```
